# 04: Eternity's Vault

## Eternity's Vault

With the tunnel location obtained, the PCs now work to build a secure vault below Luthadel—their only hope of keeping the Eternity Spike out of Gilded hands permanently.

## Running This Chapter

During this chapter, the party’s previous choices pay off, impacting the vault construction process. The ultimate fight of the Era 1 adventure follows, as Delina confronts the PCs and NeBaal’s long-held secrets finally spill over. Afterward, one final, harrowing task remains for any PCs brave enough to volunteer: NeBaal beseeches the heroes to help preserve the vault’s location, mapping out a legacy of hope for future generations to fulfill.

## Chapter Flow

To start, prepare or print the “[[Journal/17 - Appendix G Tables|Vault Traps]]” and “[[Journal/14 - Appendix D Handouts|NeBaal’s Confession]]” handouts, and prepare a copy of the “[[Journal/14 - Appendix D Handouts|My Legacy]]” handout from appendix D (or a blank piece of paper) for each player. Also prepare a copy of map 4.2; whether presented digitally or on paper, players need a way to draw on the vault layout map during this chapter.

After resolving two weeks of downtime, the PCs gather their allies to plan to for the vault’s construction. Take a moment to review “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Planning Meeting|Planning Meeting]]” and “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|Building the Vault]]” before the session.

After the planning meeting, the party must prepare for vault construction. They do so via endeavors to steal supplies from a Cett-occupied Keep Hasting and Straff Venture’s military camp. Interspersed with the action are roleplaying scenes with Guiverre and Enge.

The confrontation with Delina arrives just after the PCs finish the vault. Review “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|Confrontation]]” in advance, and look over “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Sacrifice Play|Sacrifice Play]]” and “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|NeBaal’s Confession]]” carefully, as these dramatic scenes play out the reveal of NeBaal’s true identity. Once Delina is defeated, “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#A Night to Think|A Night to Think]]” gives the PCs a chance to reflect on their legacy (which is then used in [[Journal/10 - 09 All Ends in Luthadel|chapter 9]]).

Era 1 draws to a close as the PCs decide whether to take on one last exciting—and extremely deadly—task from NeBaal in “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|Battle for Tomorrow.]]” Review “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Presenting the Battle of Luthadel|Presenting the Battle of Luthadel]]” and the Heroic Sacrifice special rule before running this section. This climactic final mission from NeBaal takes place during the Battle of Luthadel and helps direct the course of events carrying the story from Era 1 into Era 2.

## Delina’s Obsession

From her seat of power in Fellise, Delina has tracked the PCs since the ball in [[Journal/03 - 02 Behind Gilded Masks|chapter 2]], seething after the party’s narrow escape. Delina’s obsession isn’t just with the Eternity Spike, but also with “Enge” herself. Possessed with jealousy and burning with ambition, Delina needs to know the truth behind the woman claiming to be Enge—a woman whom Delina knows should be dead, and whom Vadilek ultimately rejected Delina to uphold. In this chapter, Delina will get her answers or die trying.

## Character Advancement

The Era 1 characters begin this chapter at 5th level.

Upon completing the vault, they advance to 6th level—their final advancement of the Era 1 campaign. PCs who obtained Hemalurgy notes (Guiverre’s, Vadilek’s, or Duvall’s) can progress a “Learn About Hemalurgy” goal during downtime (see “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Handling Hemalurgy|Handling Hemalurgy]]” in this book’s introduction).

## Gathering Resources

To build the vault in the tunnels below Luthadel, the PCs need to gather their allies, make a plan, and procure the necessary resources.

## Downtime in Luthadel

The chapter starts with fourteen days of downtime, while Enge painstakingly scouts the secret tunnels beneath Luthadel for an appropriate hiding place. Run this downtime using the rules in the “[[Journal/15 - Appendix E Downtime|Downtime in Luthadel]]” section of appendix E.

Let your players know this is the last downtime of the Era 1 story. This is a great moment to work with the players to complete any unfinished PC goals.

## Planning Meeting

As downtime ends, Enge calls the PCs and all their allies together for a secret meeting at Kandor Construction. Read:

The air is conspiratorial in the warehouse at Kandor Construction. It’s been patched up over the passing weeks, and the lingering stench of ash and burnt wood is barely noticeable. A large table has been dragged to the center, now covered in notes and maps and surrounded by familiar faces.

### Co-Conspirators

Describe which NPCs are attending this meeting. Anyone the PCs allied with over the course of the adventure (except for Guiverre—see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|Guiverre’s Gift]]”) could be here: Cynwise, Cadme, Anastas, Demoux, and the trusted NPCs from Kandor Constructionare all likely attendees.

Let the PCs exchange greetings with their allies. Several have updates, if present: Cadme and Anastas might talk about their secret wedding, while Kandor complains about his profit margins amid the siege. When everyone has settled down, Enge explains the plan.

### The Plan

Enge’s scouting efforts below Luthadel have revealed an ideal location to build the vault. The cave’s natural metal deposits would complicate any efforts to seek out the Eternity Spike using Allomancy alone, and the tunnels leading to the cave are labyrinthine and deep enough for an army to get lost in. It’s the perfect place to house the Spike for generations to come.

To keep the location hidden, Enge plans to swear the entire construction crew to secrecy and blindfold them. Then she and the PCs will escort them in and out of the tunnels. The process will be time-consuming, but the vault’s location must remain a guarded secret.

### Vault Resources

Enge explains the PCs need three types of resources to build and secure the vault with traps:

**Plans** determine the complexity of the traps they can build.

**Labor** determines how effectively they can build those traps.

**Materials** determine how many traps they can build.

These resource totals increase as the PCs gather allies and make preparations. Show the players the “[[Journal/17 - Appendix G Tables|Vault Traps]]” handout from appendix D, listing the traps available to add to the vault and resource costs for each. The more plans, labor, and materials the PCs amass, the more secure the final vault will be.

Over a month and a half into the siege of Luthadel, such resources are scarce, but Kandor Construction donates some starting supplies. This grants the party 5 materials, enough for a simple vault with few defenses.

### Allies and Bonuses

At this point, the allies present what they can offer:

**Demoux.** If the PCs impressed Demoux in [[Journal/04 - 03 The Many and the Few|chapter 3]], he calls on the nascent Church of the Survivor. Increase labor by 2.

**Cadme and Anastas.** If the PCs brought the lovebirds together in [[Journal/03 - 02 Behind Gilded Masks|chapter 2]], the couple scours their network for information and supplies of value. Increase both plans and materials by 1.

**Bezryl.** If Bezryl leads Kandor Construction, she works tirelessly for the PCs, researching and scouring her metalminds. Increase plans by 2.

**Kandor.** If Kandor leads Kandor Construction, he delays his own building projects to aid the party’s cause. Increase materials by 2.

**Cynwise.** If Cynwise’s worker collective leads Kandor Construction, they’re able to inspire many hands to help. Increase labor by 2.

**The Refugees.** If the PCs attempted the optional “[[Journal/15 - Appendix E Downtime#Resettling Refugees|Resettling Refugees]]” downtime activity, Aime, Roben, Tuss, and Pella pitch in. Increase labor by 1.

The PCs may have made other allies along the way; add any NPCs you feel are appropriate to this list, increasing labor by 1 for each character or group added.

### Always Another Secret

As the planning meeting winds down, Enge turns the topic toward the future. The overarching purpose of this discussion is to begin revealing NeBaal’s intention to create the Gilded Map, a key item in the Era 2 adventure.

Eternity, Enge muses, is a very long time. She’s confident the crew and vault will keep the Spike safe during their lifetimes, but what about a hundred years from now? Lives end; vaults fail; and something valuable and irreplaceable could be lost forever. So Enge confesses she’s been working on yet another plan, a secret within a secret—a clandestine way to keep the knowledge alive. A way to ensure the Spike can be watched over, protected, and perhaps even used for good someday.

Other allies, if present, share their thoughts:

- Bezryl urges the crew to preserve knowledge, arguing learning from the past is the only way to prevent it from happening again.

- Cynwise knew some of the victims, and insists their friends’ deaths have to mean more than “making some nobleborn more powerful.”

- Cadme, thinking about her days in the skaa rebellion, advises it’s never wise to entrust important secrets to just one person.

Enge invites opinions from the PCs, but as the scene closes, she is resolute: She explains she intends to find a way to preserve the location of the vault (a plan NeBaal already has well underway and may be feeling guilty for concealing; see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|NeBaal’s Confession]]” later in this chapter). Enge tells the crew she’ll be gone for a few days, but she’ll meet back up before construction is completed.

## Final Preparations

The PCs have just a few days to make preparations before vault construction begins, with opportunities to roleplay and gain resources. This section includes notable characters and factions from the Mistborn novels. If you’re looking for NPC guidance and further inspiration for running this section, see “House Cett” and “House Venture” in chapter 5 of the *Mistborn World Guide*.

These four scenes occur before “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|Building the Vault]]” and can be run in any order:

**Enge’s Modest Proposal.** If the PCs (or Enge) removed Duvall’s spike, Enge suggests using it.

**Stealing Supplies.** The PCs infi ltrate Venture’s military camp in search of supplies.

**Guiverre’s Gift.** If the PCs previously won Guiverre over, he shows up with a valuable gift.

**Keep Hasting’s Plans.** The PCs stage a scheme to steal plans from Cett’s keep.

## Enge’s Modest Proposal

This roleplaying scene is only available if Duvall was killed, resulting in either the PCs or Enge collecting Duvall’s bronze ***Hemalurgic spike***, which grants Zinc Allomancy. Enge has kept that spike safe, and she now suggests one of the PCs could put it to use. Read:

“Cards on the table, I think it’s time we fight fire with fire. It’s not like I want to do it,” Enge says quickly, grimacing. “But the Gilded Few already are. And if we don’t, we’re yet another step behind them. We need to do everything we can to protect the Eternity Spike from them.” She gestures at the spike, submerged in blood. “But it’s your call, crew. What do you say?”

If a PC takes Enge up on her offer, a character with the Hemalurgist expertise can implant the spike for them. If none of the PCs have this expertise, Enge volunteers to help.

If the PCs ask where Enge learned how to use Hemalurgy, she explains Vadilek taught her the basics. This isn’t a lie: NeBaal picked up a few tricks when working with him. But the PCs may (rightfully) doubt Enge would even suggest using a Hemalurgic spike, and a successful DC 15 Insight test reveals Enge is hiding something. If they press the point, refer to “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Handling NeBaal|Handling NeBaal]]” in this book’s introduction for guidance on how NeBaal might reveal the truth.

## Stealing Supplies

This endeavor to steal materials is introduced by the party’s favorite trusted NPC from Kandor Construction (Kandor, Bezryl, or Cynwise).

Straff Venture’s military camp sits outside the gates of Luthadel, boasting a massive and well-organized force. The army’s supplies can be repurposed as materials for vault construction, but securing them requires a multipart mission. The trusted NPC introduces this task, detailing the following obstacles:

- With two armies besieging the city and another sighted approaching, getting in and out won’t be easy.

- Venture’s camp is crawling with soldiers and obligators, and he’s even got Straff’s Mistborn (and illegitimate son) skulking about.

- The supplies are sure to be heavy and cumbersome to carry.

If the PCs accept, run the mission to steal supplies as an endeavor (detailed in “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Venturing Forth|Venturing Forth]]”). Special rules for two NPCs the party may meet, Zane and Amaranta, are active during this scene.

### Special Rule: Zane

The first time the PCs fail two tests in Straff’s camp, they come face-to-face with a bizarre young man wearing all black. All the soldiers seem afraid of him. This is **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Named/Ch 4/Zane|Zane]]** (Mistborn assassin, he/him), Straff’s Mistborn (and illegitimate son). Zane knows the PCs are meddling, but he won’t act on it unless the endeavor fails. Instead, he hangs on and asks questions, going along with whatever lies the PCs feed him like he’s in on the joke, though he occasionally seems distractedby something unseen. Zane’s presence gives PCs a +2 bonus to social tests in Straff’s camp, but should they fail the endeavor, he makes combat much harder.

### Special Rule: Amaranta

After the party’s fifth successful test, **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Named/Ch 4/Amaranta|Amaranta]]** (commoner, she/her), Straff Venture’s secret herbalist, catches them. Eager to sabotage Venture, she tells the PCs where to find some extra supply crates. The PCs have a choice to make: If they go for the extra crates, they can double the materials gathered by this endeavor, but they must earn an additional 2 successes (for a total of 8).

### Venturing Forth

To steal the supplies, the PCs must complete an endeavor (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Resolving the Endeavor|Resolving the Endeavor]]”). Obstacles the PCs might face include:

**Heavily Guarded Exits.** Covert exits are hard to find. A PC could ask around (DC 16 Persuasion) or recall rumors of passwalls leading out of the city (DC 18 Lore). If they succeed, they find a woman named **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Named/Ch 4/Granny Hilde|Granny Hilde]]** (commoner, she/her) who controls a passwall, charges 20 boxings for passage, and doesn’t ask questions.

**Patrols by the River.** To get past soldiers from both Cett’s and Venture’s armies, PCs can sneak down the riverside (DC 17 Stealth) or craft breathing devices to swim undetected (DC 16 Crafting).

**Obligators in Camp.** PCs can fashion disguises (DC 16 Thievery) or convince the obligators they belong there (DC 18 Deception or Leadership). A PC with the Steel Ministry expertise gains an advantage on this test.

**Guarded Supply Tents.** The PCs can try to pilfer the supplies sneakily (DC 16 Stealth or Thievery) or haul them out so quickly the guards can’t tell what happened (DC 18 Athletics or Discipline).

### Resolving the Endeavor

The endeavor succeeds if the party accrues 6 successes (or 8 successes if they took Amaranta’s advice) before 4 failures, or if they otherwise complete the objective.

**Success.** The PCs increase their materials by 5 (or by 10 if they took Amaranta’s advice), and the endeavor ends as the PCs escape back to Luthadel with Straff none the wiser.

**Failure.** The PCs are discovered. Three **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 1 Adversaries/Hazekiller Era 1|hazekillers]]** step forward and the endeavor ends in combat; proceed to “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Supply Scrum|Supply Scrum.]]”

### Supply Scrum

![[pg077_Map-4.1-Ventures Military Camp_Zachary Crepeau.webp]]

**Venture’s Military Camp - ZACHARY CREPEAU**

If the endeavor fails, run a combat against the three hazekillers using map 4.1. As combat begins, five 100-pound crates of materials sit nearby, marked on the map. The PCs can escape with as many crates as they can carry, or they can fight. If they fight and win, they can easily take all five crates (regardless of how many PCs are in the party). Each crate taken increases materials by 1.

If the PCs attracted Zane’s attention, he joins the fray to fight alongside the hazekillers. Zane uses the Mistborn assassin stat block, but he avoids using his Burn Atium action because the metal is so scarce. At the start of the second round, or when all the hazekillers are dead, Zane becomes distracted by a voice speaking into his mind and leaves, disappearing into the mists.

## Guiverre’s Gift

This roleplaying scene is available if the party gained Guiverre’s allegiance in [[Journal/03 - 02 Behind Gilded Masks|chapter 2]]. One night during preparations, Lord Seeris turns up unannounced. Read:

On a cold winter night, an odd sight greets you: Guiverre Seeris stands on the dock near your boat.\
While still wearing a fine coat, Guiverre’s hands are notably bare, no longer adorned with glittering rings. He looks smaller and more haggard than when you last spoke, and dark circles ring his eyes.\
“Well, come now, it’s blisteringly cold. Aren’t you going to invite me into your delightful floating… house?”

Once inside, he thanks the PCs and shares the following:

- After many weeks of Lord Seeris covertly hampering Delina’s social sway in Fellise, she grew suspicious of his allegiances. He fled to Luthadel, bribing Cett’s forces for safe passage.

- He could’ve kept running, but the PCs’ defiance inspired him. He’s decided to stay in Luthadel, doing what he can to fight for the city’s survival.

- He came to give a warning: Since the crew humiliated Delina, she’s become fixated on them. He suspects she may even be obsessed enough to make a wild move and attack them in Luthadel, siege be damned.

- If Enge is present, observant PCs notice Guiverre keeps glancing at her throughout the discussion. A successful DC 14 Insight test reveals Guiverre is both curious and concerned. If a PC follows up with Guiverre alone, he confides part of Delina’s obsession with the party: She’s absolutely certain she killed Enge… and yet Enge lives. Guiverre doesn’t know what to make of it.

Right before leaving, Guiverre produces a velvet box containing a single ***bead of atium***. His dark eyes glisten as he tells the PCs, “Protect yourselves—and get some justice for my son, if you can.”

## Keep Hasting’s Plans

This endeavor to steal plans is introduced by Cadme and Anastas if they’re present, or if not, by Enge.

Before the death of the Lord Ruler, House Hasting was one of the best in the metalworking industry. The NPC introducing the scene believes Keep Hasting’s offices may still hold blueprints for traps and hazards useful for the vault. However, Lord **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Named/Ch 4/Ashweather Cett|Ashweather Cett]]** (he/him) currently occupies the keep.

### Hatching the Scheme

With few doors and windows, all guarded by numerous soldiers, Keep Hasting is extremely difficult to infiltrate. So Cadme (or an NPC such as Kandor, who once built a vault in the keep) suggests another approach: Get Cett talking.

Cett is currently taking meetings with prominent public figures, trying to convince them to back him as king. Cadme (or Enge) can arrange a meeting with Cett and impart the information in the “Roleplaying Ashweather Cett” sidebar. Anastas can tell the party Cett was once a poet. While some PCs keep Cett distracted, other PCs can search through the keep for blueprints—in other words, the party can run a Scheme (see chapter 11 of the *Mistborn Handbook* for more on Schemes).

### Speaking With Cett

Run this scene as a conversation with Cett (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Resolving the Conversation|Resolving the Conversation]]”). This conversation scene requires splitting the party. Ask the players which PCs buy time by distracting Cett, and which PCs sneak into the keep to steal the blueprints. The spotlight then alternates between these two groups.

Successful contributions during the conversation keep Cett’s attention absorbed, making sneaking easier (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Special Rule: Sneaking|Special Rule: Sneaking]]”). However, Cett counters failed PC contributions with intimidating jabs of his own, which may push them off balance (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Resisting Influence|Resisting Influence]]”).

Once it’s decided which PCs are talking and which PCs are sneaking, begin the conversation.

### Starting the Conversation

The PCs might discuss topics such as the following:

**Discussing the Issues.** Cett is eager to talk about the changes he’ll make in Luthadel. He promises titles for everyone in the Assembly—except for the skaa, whom he’ll force back into labor without representation. If a PC who disagrees with Cett’s ideals tries to keep him talking, on a failed test, Cett realizes he’s speaking with someone who “isn’t smart enough” to understand.

**Flattery.** Cett steadfastly believes he’s meant to be king, and he agrees with anyone who flatters him. The first two times the PCs use flattery, it’s an automatic success. However, if the party continues to flatter him, Cett spends focus to resist successful tests and asks what, exactly, the party is trying to get out of him.

**Poetic Diversions.** If the PCs bring up poetry or writing, Cett jumps on the chance to show off his own work and doesn’t resist successful tests. However, if a PC fails a test, Cett brushes off any further poetic appeals, spending just 1 focus to resist the PCs’ “amateur opinions.”

**Sabotaging Venture.** If the PCs already completed the “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Stealing Supplies|Stealing Supplies]]” endeavor, they can leverage sabotaging Cett’s political rival to convince Cett they’re his allies. If the PCs offer concrete information about Venture’s campsite (such as the existence of Zane or the magnitude of Straff’s troops), they gain an advantage on this test. On a failure (or when Cett spends focus to resist a success), he replies, “The enemy of my enemy makes a lackluster friend.”

### Resisting Influence

**[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Named/Ch 4/Ashweather Cett|Ashweather Cett]]** has a Cognitive defense of 16, a Spiritual defense of 17, and 5 focus.

Cett often behaves uncouthly to set others on edge, making rude remarks about a PC’s appearance or having servants serve snacks he can eat sloppily to disgust his guests. After a failed contribution, Cett uses his Crass Remark ability to keep the PCs off balance.

### Special Rule: Sneaking

Every contribution to the conversation influences Cett’s perception of events, and in turn, how alert his guards are. After every contribution to the conversation, move the spotlight back to the sneaking PCs, and have each sneaking PC make a Stealth test. The DC is determined by how well the last contribution went over, as shown on the Stealth Test DC table.

#### [[Journal/17 - Appendix G Tables|Stealth Test DC]]

On a failure, the sneaking PC is discovered by the guards and kicked out. If all the sneaking PCs are discovered, the conversation fails (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Resolving the Conversation|Resolving the Conversation]]”).

On a success, the PC can search a room in the keep. To represent this, have them roll 1d6:

**On a 6**, they locate and acquire the blueprints they need, which brings the scene to a successful close (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Resolving the Conversation|Resolving the Conversation]]”).

**On a 5 or lower**, they find nothing and the conversation must continue to distract Cett from the sneaking PCs. However, the next time a PC makes this roll to search a room, they locate the blueprints on either a 6 or a 5 (and the next time, on a 6, 5, or 4—and so on, until success).

### Resolving the Conversation

The conversation continues until either the sneaking PCs find what they need, all the sneaking PCs are discovered, Cett runs out of focus, or all the PCs distracting Cett run out of focus.

If Cett runs out of focus, he becomes belligerent, grows tired of the conversing PCs’ games, and kicks them out. If the PCs distracting Cett all run out of focus, they become too angry or lose their nerve, and the party must leave. In either case, the sneaking PCs can each immediately roll 1d6 to search one final room, no Stealth test required.

The conversation succeeds if a sneaking PC finds the blueprints before the conversation ends.

**Success.** The scheme succeeds, increasing the party’s plans by 2. Additionally, if there are two or more PCs still sneaking around by the time they find the blueprints, they also find a set of wooden halfplate armor. The party is free to end the conversation and leave at any time.

**Failure.** If the scheme fails, one **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 1 Adversaries/Enforcer Era 1|enforcer]]** and three **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 1 Adversaries/Garrisoner Era 1|garrisoners]]** drag any PCs who were still sneaking into the dining room where Cett and the rest of the party are conversing. Combat ensues as Cett’s guards attack the party, and any PCs who were already kicked out can choose to burst into the hall at the start of the first round. However, it quickly becomes apparent to the PCs that this fight isn’t winnable, and three more garrisoners appear at the start of each round after the first, until the party escapes.

## Building the Vault

Once preparations are completed, vault construction begins in earnest. The heroes, Enge, and their blindfolded construction crew carry their supplies deep within the secret Ministry tunnels. After miles of navigating, Enge points out the spot. Read:

At last, the dark, twisting tunnels open into a spacious cavern complex. Metal deposits on the ceilings gleam in your torchlight. Down below, past the steep slopes, a rushing waterfall empties into a pool of murky, ashy water.

## Cavern Features

Since the party is building their vault within an existing cavern structure, that structure’s features can help or hinder construction. The cavern includes the following:

**Two Entrances.** There are two entrances to the vault space, leading into areas T1 and T3.

**Running Water.** There’s an underground waterfall here. Building traps in area T6 is challenging: Increase the trap’s material requirement by 1 and its DC by 3.

**Slopes and Cliffs.** These caverns contain several steep 20-foot cliffs, splitting the caves into two elevations. Areas T3, T6, and S1 lie at the lower elevation. Building within 5 feet of the cliffs increases the trap’s material requirement by 1 and its DC by 2.

## Placing the Spike

The PCs start the build by making a critical placement decision: where the protective case for the Eternity Spike should be secured into the wall. Rich veins of metal deposits run through caverns S1 and S2; cavern S1 lies behind the waterfall. The PCs must pick one of these two locations.

## Building Traps

In this scene, the PCs take turns populating the vault layout map with as many vault traps and trap modifications as possible (refer to the “[[Journal/17 - Appendix G Tables|Vault Traps]]” handout from appendix D for the full list of available traps). While the players should discuss their plans as a group, let a different player serve as the final decisionmaker for each turn.

To place each trap, follow these steps:

1.  **Choose a Trap.** The player chooses a trap or trap modification from the “[[Journal/17 - Appendix G Tables|Vault Traps]]” handout to install. Ensure any trap prerequisites are met.

2.  **Decide Placement.** The player indicates where on the vault layout map they plan to install this trap.

3.  **Deduct Material Cost.** Deduct the trap’s associated material cost from the party’s resource totals.

4.  **Expend Labor.** A player can optionally expend labor points to reduce the trap’s associated DC by 2 for each point expended. The player must decide how much labor (if any) to expend before rolling, deducting it from the party’s resource totals.

5.  **Attempt the Build.** Ask your player to describe how they’re building their chosen trap. What materials is it made of? If they expended labor, which NPC do they rely on for help? To determine whether the build was successful, have the PC make a Crafting, Athletics, Discipline, Leadership, or Survival test against the trap’s associated DC (be sure to reduce the difficulty if labor was spent). If the trap’s prerequisites included Plans 2 or more, raise the stakes on the test.

6.  **Mark the Map.** If the build test succeeds, the player draws the trap on the vault layout map. If the build test fails, they still draw the failed trap on the map—but they should explain how the build failed, and describe the resulting mess of materials. The next test made to build that same trap gains an advantage.

Materials and labor are expended whether the build test succeeds or fails. The PCs should take turns building vault traps and drawing on the map until they run out of materials, at which point construction is finished.

### Delina’s Tactics

During the fight, Delina pulls no punches. In this moment, make the PCs aware they must kill or be killed.

Delina has one bead of atium, which she burns as soon as possible. Encourage players who gain an Opportunity to spend it to end the effects of her Burn Atium.

While Delina fights the PCs, she Banters (0) every round, trying every manipulation tactic she can think of, including:

**Threatening the Party.** To Enge, “I’m going to kill your so-called friends, one by one. So you can watch, or you can save them by coming with me.”

**Manipulation.** To the PCs, “I knew Engeanthe, I trained with her for years. She was my friend. Obviously none of you even knew her!”

**Sowing Mistrust.** To the PCs, “Whoever that is, it isn’t Engeanthe. I beat the life out of Engeanthe myself. I watched the light leave her eyes. That impostor is playing you for fools!”

“Delina, take me—spare them.” Not-Enge steps forward, surrendering. With no further use for you, Delina walks the impostor off the docks. In a heartbeat, the two are gone.

## NeBaal's Confession

In the wake of the confrontation with Delina, the PCs are left with a canal boat full of secrets. If the PCs didn’t guess Enge’s real identity before now, it’s time for the reveal.

If NeBaal is here, they tell the PCs the full truth. They openly answer the party’s questions, giving the answers below, and they offer gifts for the PCs (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|NeBaal’s Final Gifts]]”). They also request the PCs take on one last, deadly mission.

If NeBaal was captured, the PCs find a letter left on the canal boat, laid out next to NeBaal’s gifts (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|NeBaal’s Final Gifts]]”). You can give the players the “[[Journal/14 - Appendix D Handouts|NeBaal’s Letter]]” handout found in appendix D, or you can read the following conversation quotes, which form the same letter:

**Letter Opening.** “If you’re reading this, I am gone. After everything you’ve done to help me, you deserve an explanation.”

**Who are you really?** “My real name is NeBaal. I’m a kandra, and my Contract was with Vadilek. He tortured thousands of people, then left them to die, but I was bound to serve him. So I did. I was charged to watch over his niece. Thus, I had the honor of knowing Enge—and all of you.”

**Why impersonate Enge?** “Vadilek did one thing right: He saw Enge’s promise. But the only thing she shared with her uncle was her passion. She was good. I watched her grow up. And when Vadilek lured her back in, I watched Delina kill her. I grieved for Enge, and then I became her.”

**How could you lie to us?** “I understand if you feel betrayed. While I deceived you, you gave me a gift. Over these past months, I got to see the world through Enge’s eyes. I saw how much you all loved her, and I felt how much she loved all of you. How much we loved you. I am only sorry I didn’t tell you the truth sooner.”

**What about the Eternity Spike? What happens now?** “For now, the Spike is secure—but when you live as long as I have, you realize how uncertain our future truly is. I’m not really your boss, so I have no right to ask, but one last smuggling job awaits this crew. I’ve created a map pointing to the vault’s location, so we may safeguard the Spike for the future…no matter what happens to me, or you. I’ve split the map into two pieces and made arrangements with trusted contacts in Tresting to ensure those pieces are kept safe—by very different groups of people. I hope to ensure all of humankind, not just one individual, decides how the Eternity Spike’s story ends. But first, we must smuggle the map pieces safely out of the city, beyond the grasp of the Gilded Few and those invading Luthadel.”

**Is this map/job the only way?** “I wish I did not have to ask this of you. The job will be incredibly difficult, made deadly by the siege, and you might die doing it. But I trust you—just as Enge trusted you. Vadilek was her blood, but you were her family. And this map is my last, best hope. Perhaps one day, humankind will learn. Perhaps the future will be kinder. In a world without the Gilded Few, the Spike could be used for good. Or perhaps knowledge of the Metallic Arts will grow until someone can finally destroy the Spike. Either way, the map must survive, to secure the future of the Spike—and the future of the world.”

**And if we say no?** “Please, do not make this decision lightly. Take the night to think it over. Refusing is certainly the safer option. But if you choose to help, rest well tonight, and leave before the dawn.”

**Letter Closing.** “I leave you with some final gifts. I hope you’ll accept them and carry the map to safety. I hope you’ll survive, and earn a chance to lay down your burdens—as I have, perhaps, finally laid down mine. I may be gone, but for everything you’ve done, for everything you’ve given, and for giving Enge a true family—I love you. Always. Yours with eternal gratitude, NeBaal.”

## Red Dawn in Luthadel

Run this scene to carry the map pieces out of Luthadel as an endeavor (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Resolving the Endeavor|Resolving the Endeavor]]”). The PCs must navigate their way through a city falling to a koloss attack while also avoiding opportunistic thieves and Gilded Few adversaries.

### Special Rule: Heroic Sacrifice

Escaping Luthadel is a deadly endeavor. If a PC fails a test to progress the endeavor, they die. However, a second PC (or NeBaal) can make a Heroic Sacrifice, giving their life to save their crewmate. If they do, the sacrificed character dies instead of the PC who failed the test, and that failure turns into a success. If NeBaal is with the party, they always make a Heroic Sacrifice on the first PC failure.

When a PC dies, ask the player, “What is the last thing the rest of the party sees as you stand against impossible odds?” Allow them to narrate their own heroic last stand.

If the last PC is about to die, the endeavor fails; refer to “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Resolving the Endeavor|Resolving the Endeavor.]]”

### Running the Endeavor

Run this endeavor as a series of hazards the PCs must overcome on their way out of the falling city. Choose hazards to present from the following list (but be sure to save “Leaving the City” and “Army Soldiers” for last, and run those two hazards in that order):

**Gilded Few Assassins.** A pair of elite Gilded Few assassins leading a contingent of fifty house guards spots the PCs and demands the party give up the Eternity Spike. A PC might use their knowledge of Luthadel to lose the enemies in the chaos (DC 20 Survival), threaten their leaders (DC 22 Intimidation), or sow dissent in the ranks with emotional Allomancy. Social tests against the Gilded Few gain a disadvantage.

**Koloss Marauders.** Massive blue-skinned attackers block the street, pounding on a barricaded tenement door while skaa scream inside. A PC might find another route (DC 17 Deduction) or draw the koloss away and try to outrun them (DC 22 Athletics). This is a good place to raise the stakes.

**Koloss Scouts.** A roaming band of howling koloss catches sight of the party, chasing them into an abandoned warehouse. A PC might put together a quick trap (DC 18 Crafting), conceal the party until the koloss lose interest (DC 17 Thievery), or spot a narrow escape path the koloss can’t fit through (DC 18 Perception). This is a good place to raise the stakes.

**Press-Ganged.** A panicking noble hides in his carriage as his soldiers force terrifi ed skaa in between them and the oncoming koloss, “recruiting them for duty.” The noble orders the PCs to protect him and his soldiers—a potentially deadly and time-consuming prospect. A PC might assure the skaa they don’t need to listen (DC 20 Leadership), threaten to report the noble’s lawless “recruitment” to an army commander (DC 18 Lore), or give false information to send the noble and soldiers packing (DC 17 Deception).

**Leaving the City.** Having navigated through the streets of Luthadel, the PCs must now exit the city. A PC could find a secure area to climb (DC 20 Athletics), fi nd a passwall that hasn’t yet been shut down (DC 22 Survival), sneak past frenzied koloss at a fallen gate (DC 22 Stealth), or bribe an overwhelmed noble into opening an intact gate long enough for the party—and a crowd of desperate civilians—to escape (DC 20 Leadership or Persuasion).

**Army Soldiers.** Outside Luthadel, the party is confronted by a scouting group from the Venture or Cett army. The scouts are in rough shape, having just survived an encounter with some koloss. A PC could avoid being detained by this group by tending to their wounded (DC 17 Medicine), convincing them another fight isn’t worth the trouble (DC 18 Intimidation), or even inspiring them to assist and accompany the party (DC 24 Leadership).

### Resolving the Endeavor

As the surviving PCs approach Tresting, read:

Ash falls from the sky. After a harrowing journey, you follow directions to a modest skaa hovel outside of Tresting. Inside is a warm cook fire, the glow illuminating familiar faces.

There to greet the PCs are two familiar NPCs. These NPCs have agreed to work with NeBaal to hide and protect the map. At NeBaal’s request, they took a passwall out of Luthadel a few days prior; when they did, none of them had realized the siege was about to boil over.

Which NPCs NeBaal chose depends on who the party trusted most. The first NPC must be one of the party’s trusted NPCs from Kandor Construction. The other should be one of the following NPCs the characters were close to—just be sure to choose NPCs who would take the map pieces to different locations:

**Bezryl.** Bezryl would take a piece of the map to Tathingdwen.

**Cynwise or Cadme.** Cynwise or Cadme would stay in the skaa villages near Tresting.

**Guiverre or Anastas.** Anastas or Guiverre would take the map piece to Fadrex City.

### Closing Narration

If the PCs succeeded in the “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Red Dawn in Luthadel|Red Dawn in Luthadel]]” endeavor, take a moment to linger on these characters as they part ways, describing the trusted NPCs leaving with the map pieces and the PCs staying behind—either with NeBaal by their side, or holding the Midnight Mistcloak in NeBaal’s memory.

If the PCs failed, instead take a moment to stay with the last surviving PC, accompanied by two friends who sit with them as they succumb to their wounds. What last request do they ask of these trusted NPCs?

Either way, if any individual PCs stayed behind and rejected NeBaal’s mission, give them the opportunity now to describe how they either survived or succumbed to the siege of Luthadel, as an epilogue.

## Journeying Onward in the New Empire

This is the end of *Mistborn Legacy’s* Era 1 adventure. As the book closes on the Era 1 PCs, a new story is about to begin. To start the Era 2 campaign and allow the Era 1 story to echo out across time, complete the following GM preparation tasks:

- For each PC who died during the Battle of Luthadel (or for whom you established some other death during the session), have the player record how and when their PC died on their “My Legacy” handout.

- Save both the vault layout map the players drew on and the “My Legacy” handouts the players wrote on; these will be important to refer back to during the Era 2 campaign.

- Guide your players in creating their new Era 2 characters. These character start at 3rd level; refer to “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction|Creating Characters]]” in this book’s introduction for full instructions.

- During Era 2 character creation, provide your players the “[[Journal/14 - Appendix D Handouts|Elendel Daily Broadsheet]]” handout, and discuss the changes Scadrial has seen since the Era 1 adventure. Emphasize the shift in tone and atmosphere in Era 2; the “Using the Eras” section in chapter 13 of the *Mistborn Handbook* offers guidance on what to highlight.

- Encourage players to consider how their characters relate to their Era 1 counterparts: Do they view Era 1 characters as ancient history, romantic legend, or a personal legacy to honor… or perhaps defy?

When you’ve finished these preparations and the players have their Era 2 characters ready, continue to chapter 5, “[[Journal/06 - 05 Engines and Espionage|Engines and Espionage.]]”
