# 00: Introduction

## Introduction

On Scadrial, there’s always another secret. In the shadows and mists of this world, cunning adversaries gather, conducting dreadexperiments powerful enough to change the course of history.

*Mistborn Legacy* tells the tale of the Eternity Spike—an artifact with horrific origins and mysterious, unexplored potential—and the generational obsession surrounding it. This is a story in two parts, about greedy souls reaching for Ascension and immortality, and about the two groups of heroes who oppose them, separated by passing centuries and a changing world. This introduction provides an overview of the adventure, guidance for running it, and suggestions for tying the characters into an epic story spanning two eras.

## About this Book

*Mistborn Legacy* is an adventure optimized for four player characters (PCs). This cast rotates halfway through the adventure. In Era 1, the heroes thwart a dangerous secret society, and in Era 2, a second group of heroes clash with an insidious new generation of villains—and across time, the story all swirls around the Eternity Spike. In total, this sweeping adventure spans nine chapters. The first four tell the Era 1 story, bringing PCs from level 1 to 6. The Era 2 story spans the last five chapters, taking the PCs from level 3 to 9, with the option to continue beyond the adventure to level 10.

When a section or rule applies to a single era, this book denotes that with a symbol: Era 1 for Era 1, and Era 2 for Era 2.

Besides this book, you’ll need a copy of the *Mistborn Handbook* to play Mistborn Legacy.

## Welcome to Scadrial

A world of mists and manipulative gods, Scadrial is the setting of Brandon Sanderson’s Mistborn series, filled with opportunities for adventure. Era 1 showcases themes of intrigue, survival, and revolution. In Era 2, technology and progress drive the action in a world remade. In every era, the Metallic Arts are vitally important to the world and its people.

## Era 1: The Final Empire Era 1

The Final Empire was a thousand-year hegemony controlled by an immortal emperor, the Lord Ruler. The Era 1 chapters of *Mistborn Legacy* are set in 1024 FE, a year after the revolution that dethroned and killed the Lord Ruler. Key details about this time include the following:

**Fall of the Lord Ruler.** During the Survivor’s revolution, a Mistborn named Vin defeated the Lord Ruler. Luthadel is now administered by King Elend Venture and the Assembly, a three-part legislative body including representatives from the nobility, the merchants, and the skaa.

**Skaa Freedom.** The skaa underclass was liberated, and Luthadel has become a city where skaa can pursue business, art, religion, and other passions freely. Major skaa factions include the Church of the Survivor and the growing merchant class.

**Noble Houses.** The Great Houses fled the capital or were killed when the Lord Ruler fell. Smaller houses joined the new government or found other ways to survive. Several notable non-player characters (NPCs) in this adventure hail from houses Elariel, Haught, Seeris, and Tekiel.

**The Steel Ministry.** Some obligators renounced their faith after the Lord Ruler fell and continued on in Luthadel, while others left with the Great Houses. Further afield, some work to continue the Lord Ruler’s legacy.

**The Terris People.** Keepers spread their knowledge openly throughout the former empire. However, many stewards return to the Terris Dominance.

## Era 2: The Elendel Basin Era 2

Three hundred years have passed since the world was remade by a new god, Harmony. The Ashmounts are dormant, and society moves ever forward. The Era 2 chapters of *Mistborn Legacy* are set in 345 PC, a few years after the recovery of the Bands of Mourning and the revelation of the Malwish and the populations of Southern Scadrial. Key details about this time include the following:

**Rise of Technology.** Technological wonders prevail. Nobles drive prototype motorcars in street races, mass-printed broadsheets spread the news, and the radio technology once held only by secretive organizations is being used in new, innovative ways.

**High Society.** The descendants of the Final Empire’s noble houses still hold disproportionate power in the Elendel Basin. Notable adversaries in this adventure hail from houses Conrad, Elariel, and Tekiel.

**The Roughs.** A frontier of freedom, open spaces, and—often—lawlessness, the Roughs produces raw materials for the Basin under the yoke of mining and transport companies. The region is home to the Ironbrand outlaws and the Wilko koloss clan.

## Metallic Arts

To advance their agendas, heroes and villains alike use Metallic Arts:

**Allomancy.** By ingesting specifi c metals to power their abilities, Allomancers can manipulate emotions, move metals without touching them, and more.

**Feruchemy.** Using specific metals to store attributes, Feruchemists can become stronger, faster, and even think more quickly.

**Hemalurgy.** A forbidden Metallic Art, Hemalurgy uses metal spikes to steal a trait or power from a victim—usually killing them in the process—and grant the stolen power to another.

For more information about Scadrial, its history, and its Metallic Arts, refer to Welcome to Scadrial and the Mistborn World Guide.

## Playing With Darker Themes

*Mistborn Legacy* is an action-packed, time-hopping adventure in a morally gray world rife with horror elements. A non-exhaustive list of content warnings includes blood, gore, body horror, human experimentation, self-harm (cult sacrifice), implied torture, allusions to suicide, eugenics, racism, extreme classism (such as encountering actions that dehumanize people living in poverty), hazing, and a gas attack against civilians.

Given these darker themes, you should let your players know what to expect before starting and check in with everyone’s comfort level surrounding the elements above. Chapter 13 of the *Mistborn Handbook* offers a helpful “Safety Guide” section with guidance on how to identify which topics are comfortable for your players (and which ones aren’t), so gameplay is enjoyable and safe for everyone.

## Handling Canon

*Mistborn Legacy* touches on several canon characters, locations, and events. The “Canon and Continuity” section in chapter 13 of the *Mistborn Handbook* discusses different methods of handling canon.

### The Final Empire Era 1

Some of this adventure directly overlaps with events in *The Well of Ascension*. If your group wishes to maintain continuity with the books, the Era 1 portion of *Mistborn Legacy* engages with canon characters and events at the following points:

**Chapter 1 (1024 FE, Summer).** A year after the Lord Ruler’s death, the adventure starts in Urteau, which is a noble stronghold still ruled by Straff Venture. Shortly after the PCs leave Urteau, Straff Venture begins his march on Luthadel.

**Chapter 2 (1024 FE, Autumn).** The PCs attend a secret ball in Fellise (as Elend has ended balls in Luthadel). Attendees mentioned in the books include Kliss, Lord Haltier Habren, Triss Urbain, and Yestal Haught. As the chapter ends, Straff Venture’s troops are spotted and the Siege of Luthadel begins.

**Chapter 3 (1024 FE, Autumn).** Ashweather Cett’s army joins the siege, Breeze returns to Luthadel, and resources are tight. Straff Venture makes a test attack on the city, just as the PCs start their investigation for Kandor. Demoux preaches about the Survivor.

**Chapter 4 (1024 FE, Winter).** Elend receives a noconfi dence vote, the Assembly discusses electing a new king, and Cett enters the city, occupying Keep Hasting. The party raids camp Venture while Zane, Straff, and Amaranta reside there, and the PCs meet with Cett the same week Vin and Elend do. As the party’s vault construction begins, Penrod is elected and Cett withdraws from the city. The PCs fight Delina the eve before the Battle of Luthadel, and they smuggle the Gilded Map out of the city as the epic battle begins.

### Post-Catacendre Era 2

The Era 2 portion of *Mistborn Legacy* falls in the spring of 345 PC between *The Bands of Mourning* and *The Lost Metal*. No major canon events occur during the Era 2 arc of the adventure.

## Traversing the Time Skip

*Mistborn Legacy* is a pair of interconnected campaigns played across two time periods. The choices the PCs make during the Era 1 story are reflected in the Era 2 story, allowing players to organically contribute to a narrative reverberating three centuries forward in time.

Watch for “Time Skip Connection” sidebars highlighting important moments where the two stories intersect. PC legacies (see “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Character Legacies|Character Legacies]]” later in this introduction) also interlink the eras, enabling the Era 1 PCs to shape the future of Scadrial—and the backstories of the Era 2 PCs. Every group of players will make different choices during Era 1, which means each Era 2 party begins the story with different legacy rewards to inherit.

Three events must always happen during Era 1 to support the story in Era 2:

1.  NeBaal must be captured by the Gilded Few (usually in [[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|chapter 4]]).

2.  The Eternity Spike must be hidden in the vault (in [[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|chapter 4]]).

3.  The map pieces must survive the Battle of Luthadel (in [[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|chapter 4]]) and, eventually, the Catacendre.

## Playing With Metalborn

Chapter 5 of the *Mistborn Handbook* provides full rules and GM guidance for playing with Metalborn PCs. The following sections provide additional advice specific to *Mistborn Legacy*.

### Training and Constructing in *MISTBORN LEGACY*

Subject to the usual restrictions (era and metal availability), characters in *Mistborn Legacy* have abundant access to mentors, metals, and training in Metalborn powers.

In Era 1, the Lord Ruler has fallen and the Steel Ministry is scattered. Terris Feruchemists practice their arts openly, and skaa Mistings no longer need to hide.

In Era 2, there are no longer any Mistborn or full Feruchemists, but Mistings, Ferrings, and Twinborn can easily find mentors and materials.

### Metallic Arts in Action

Metalborn powers are useful in a wide range of scenes, not just combat. Allow your players to creatively use their Metallic Arts for their contributions during endeavors and conversations.

## Playing With Kandra and Koloss-Blooded

*Mistborn Legacy* was designed with humans, kandra, and koloss-blooded PCs in mind, and players are encouraged to freely choose from these ancestries during this adventure. (As always, the koloss-blooded ancestry is only available in Era 2.)

In general, this adventure strives to present scenarios where a character’s ancestry, culture, or legacy adds texture and nuance to the story. Playing a human, kandra, or koloss-blooded character changes gameplay, but no ancestry should significantly limit a character’s overall ability to participate in or impact the game. Consult the “Safety Guide” in chapter 13 of the *Mistborn Handbook* for guidance on integrating kandra and koloss-blooded PCs into your campaign in considerate ways.

### Kandra in *MISTBORN LEGACY*

For GMs, kandra PCs present unique challenges—and opportunities to craft incredible, memorable story beats. Consider the following when planning to run *Mistborn Legacy*.

#### Impersonating the Villains

*Mistborn Legacy* offers several opportunities for a kandra PC to obtain an enemy’s bones and impersonate them. However, the kandra PC won’t have a month to observe or research the adversaries in this campaign (such as Duvall, Delina, and the Gilded Trio). This limitation means kandra PCs must make the usual Deception tests to maintain their disguises (see the Kandra Disguise talent in chapter 2 of the *Mistborn Handbook*).

#### Interacting With NeBaal

A kandra PC has no special abilities to sense NeBaal or their deception. However, NeBaal may reveal their ruse early to that PC, hoping to find an empathetic ear. See “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Handling NeBaal|Handling NeBaal]]” for more guidance.

In Era 2, a kandra PC could help NeBaal acclimate to the new world in [[Journal/07 - 06 Desire of a Hungry Soul|chapter 6]] and beyond.

#### Kandra Immortality

Kandra are functionally immortal, so it’s possible for a single kandra PC to appear in both eras’ stories. Though the PCs advance to level 6 during the Era 1 campaign, a recurring kandra PC should begin Era 2 at level 3 (like the other PCs). This could reflect the kandra being out of practice, taking on an entirely new identity, or experiencing memory loss after spending time without their spikes. Similarly, the kandra shouldn’t know what (or where) the Gilded Map pieces are before the adventure begins. Work with the player to determine how their kandra PC lost track of this information over the past three centuries.

#### Kandra in Era 1

The Era 1 chapters take place a year after the death of the Lord Ruler. During this time, the kandra are isolated in their homeland. If you’re following Era 1 canon, most kandra only legally leave their homeland under a Contract, which (among other rules) always stipulates they may not kill or harm humans.

Enge could hold the PC’s Contract, having “borrowed” from an Elariel atium cache and contracted a kandra spy for her smuggling operation. Alternatively, a noble house, such as Erikell or Hasting, may have heard rumors of the Gilded Few and sent a kandra to keep an eye on Vadilek’s wayward niece.

#### Kandra in Era 2

The Era 2 chapters cover a time in Scadrian history when the kandra are encouraged to pursue their own interests, which could organically lead them to the party. Alternatively, a kandra in Era 2 who joins the party might have been asked by Harmony to keep a wary eye on the activities of the Gilded Trio. If so, if Ironeyes approaches the party in [[Journal/09 - 08 Fatal Fashion|chapter 8]] or [[Journal/10 - 09 All Ends in Luthadel|chapter 9]], he may see the kandra as an associate or aide.

### Suggested Kandra Legacies

To seamlessly incorporate kandra PCs into *Mistborn Legacy*, this adventure’s PC legacies (see “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Character Legacies|Character Legacies]]”) require some modification. Consider the following for inspiration.

#### Era 1: Official

Establish when and why the kandra PC took an obligator’s bones. Many crews (Enge’s smuggling operation included) could find use for an obligator spy. If the PC only spent a short time impersonating the obligator before the Empire’s fall, replace the question “What caused you to recognize the weight of the injustices committed by the Ministry or your institution?” with the question “What hard decision were you forced to make in an official capacity, and why does it still weigh on you?”

#### Era 1: Veteran

Establish when and why the kandra PC took a famous war hero’s bones. Was it out of sentimentality, practicality, or random chance? If Enge contracted the PC, the Veteran’s visage may be helpful for missions. If the PC is spying on Enge, the Veteran comes with a trustworthy reputation.

During [[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|chapter 4]], the Veteran recognizes an NPC soldier. If the PC took the bones after the Veteran left the military, modify this moment: Ask the player how their kandra PC would know this NPC.

#### Era 2: Explorer

An Explorer kandra may have lost their memories due to removing their spikes, either during the Resolution or otherwise. Now they hope to rediscover their own history via the Chronicler’s record. Replace the question “Why are you currently so focused on the Chronicler’s record?” with the question “How did you lose and regain your spikes?”

#### Era 2: Lawkeeper

A Lawkeeper kandra may weigh their responsibility to uphold the Official’s philosophy against their duties to Harmony. Replace the question “How does your work ethic or theory of justice draw inspiration from (or reject) the ethos of the Official?” with the question “How do you view upholding the law and the Official’s ethos, in light of your service to Harmony?”

#### Other Legacies

In addition to the legacies mentioned here, the Era 1 Chronicler and Convict and the Era 2 Storyteller can all be used for kandra PCs without requiring major modifications to their legacy sidebars or questions.

## Handling Hemalurgy

Hemalurgy plays a significant role in *Mistborn Legacy*, and uncovering knowledge about Hemalurgy is a major achievement for the PCs in both eras. Unless otherwise noted, follow the rules in the “Hemalurgic Spikes” section in chapter 8 of the *Mistborn Handbook*. Using Hemalurgic spikes and understanding Hemalurgy requires completing a goal to obtain the Hemalurgist specialist expertise.

When planning how to approach Hemalurgy in your game, consider referring to the “Safety Guide” in chapter 13 of the Mistborn Handbook (especially the “Violence, Gore, and Horror” section).

### Hemalurgic Texts in *MISTBORN LEGACY*

PCs may find Hemalurgic texts in the following chapters:

**[[Journal/02 - 01 Echoes of the Lost|Chapter 1]].** Vadilek’s own Hemalurgy notes can be found in a hidden safe in the Secret Crucible.

**[[Journal/03 - 02 Behind Gilded Masks|Chapter 2]].** The party may leave with Guiverre’s Hemalurgy notes.

**[[Journal/04 - 03 The Many and the Few|Chapter 3]].** When the party defeats Duvall, they find his Hemalurgy notes.

**[[Journal/06 - 05 Engines and Espionage|Chapter 5]].** The heisted crate contains the Era 1 party’s Hemalurgy notes.

**[[Journal/07 - 06 Desire of a Hungry Soul|Chapter 6]].** There are no notes in this chapter, but PCs who see the anatomical diagram in “Induction” have a chance to immediately progress their goals.

**[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil|Chapter 7]].** Bayron’s “Killer Deal” includes his Hemalurgy notes.

### Learning About Hemalurgy

During downtime, a PC can spend seven days studying a Hemalurgic text, making a DC 15 Lore or Medicine test. On a success, the PC understands the text and can progress a “Learn About Hemalurgy” goal. On a failure, they don’t progress the goal, but they gain an advantage on this test the next time they study the same text.

#### Hemalurgy Milestones in Era 1

In Era 1, the goal can only be progressed once per Hemalurgic text.

#### Hemalurgy Milestones in Era 2

In Era 2, PCs find the Era 1 party’s Hemalurgy notes and benefit from their research. After successfully studying these notes, each Era 2 PC can mark as many milestones as the Era 1 PCs ultimately did. If an Era 1 PC achieved the “Learn About Hemalurgy” goal, an Era 2 PC can, after spending seven days successfully studying the Era 1 PC’s notes, mark all remaining milestones and complete the “Learn About Hemalurgy” goal.

Bayron’s Hemalurgy notes (acquired if PCs take Bayron’s “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#A Killer Deal|Killer Deal]]” in chapter 7) are similarly extensive enough for the Era 2 PCs to complete the “Learn About Hemalurgy” goal by successfully studying them.

#### Secrets of the Spikes

As a PC marks Hemalurgy milestones in either era, they progressively learn the following:

**First Milestone.** The Ascension ritual requires two different types of spikes: the Eternity Spike and an Inquisitor-esque latticework of spikes.

**Second Milestone.** The Eternity Spike contains massive amounts of energy. One usage is the Ascension ritual—but could it be used for another purpose?

**Third Milestone.** The ritual seems to contain a mathematical contradiction or mistake Vadilek couldn’t solve. This flaw is likely why the beast the Era 1 PCs found in the lab wasn’t human anymore.

### Unlocking Expertise

A character who completes the “Learn About Hemalurgy” goal gains the Hemalurgist specialist expertise from the *Mistborn Handbook*. In the *Mistborn Legacy* adventure, this usually happens during down time in [[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|chapter 4]].

## Handling NeBaal

In Era 1, NeBaal is disguised as Enge. NeBaal is meant to be a flawed but ultimately sympathetic character, and the party’s discovery of the kandra’s true identity is a plot point intended to reward engaged, observant players. NeBaal’s identity is always revealed by the end of [[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|chapter 4]], but if your players grow suspicious, NeBaal’s confession may come early. This section outlines clues to foreshadow the twist and guidance for handling an early reveal (see “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Coming Clean|Coming Clean]]”).

### Hints to Drop

NeBaal is a practiced impersonator with centuries of Contract experience, and they spent years watching over Enge. Since Vadilek sent NeBaal to spy on Enge and the PCs, NeBaal even knows the party’s dynamics and personal backgrounds. There aren’t many breaks in the kandra’s act.

Still, reveals are more fun when you keep the players guessing. Consider dropping the following hints:

**Surprising Survival.** Most NPCs assume Enge is dead; Delina saw her die. NeBaal claims Delina attacked Enge with the Eternity Spike, badly injuring her, but Vadilek prevented a killing blow. To sell the story to the PCs, “Enge” says she escaped using her signature “You Just Mist Me” maneuver.

**Missing Allomancy.** Kandra can’t use Allomancy. Enge avoids missions requiring her to be the group’s Smoker. If asked, Enge confesses she can’t feel her metal reserves, speculating the Eternity Spike stole her Allomancy.

**Staying Secluded.** NeBaal hangs back on the canal boat during [[Journal/03 - 02 Behind Gilded Masks|chapter 2]] and always avoids contact with the Gilded Few. Though Enge has reason to lie low, she also claims to feel weak and unfocused due to her injury, as if the Eternity Spike sapped her stamina.

**Forgotten Secrets.** NeBaal doesn’t know some secrets shared only with Enge, blaming missing memories on their injury.

If a PC has reason to become suspicious, have them make an Insight test against NeBaal. The DC for this test depends on the current chapter: DC 25 for chapter 1, DC 24 for chapter 2, DC 22 for chapter 3, or DC 20 for chapter 4. On a failure, Enge seems truthful, though the PC detects she’s hiding sadness and fear.

On a success, they know Enge isn’t telling the whole story, and they might identify another hint or clue about NeBaal’s secrets. Refer to “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Coming Clean|Coming Clean]]” if the PC keeps pressing.

### Clues to Find

If the PCs successfully spy on Enge, they might find the following:

**[[Journal/03 - 02 Behind Gilded Masks|Chapter 2]].** Among Enge’s personal effects are scribbled notes about what to do with the Eternity Spike, containing obsessive, repeated queries about what “she” would’ve wanted. If asked, Enge claims “she” was her mother.

**[[Journal/04 - 03 The Many and the Few|Chapter 3]].** Enge has the idea of building a vault in mind well before the PCs head to Kandor Construction. Notes planning how to acquire supplies can be found in her quarters, showing her strategizing to protect the Spike alone, long before explaining her ideas to the party.

**[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|Chapter 4]].** During downtime at the start of [[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|chapter 4]], Enge creates most of the Gilded Map. She can be observed meeting with Cadme to acquire the mask or collecting the Unity Medal from a trusted NPC at Kandor Construction. These items can alternatively be found by PCs searching her possessions. After the planning meeting, Enge can be found searching for a passwall or making arrangements for the map pieces in Tresting (see “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault#Hope for a Kinder Future|Hope for a Kinder Future]]” in chapter 4). NeBaal immediately comes clean if confronted about these chapter 4 clues.

### Coming Clean

If the players don’t accept NeBaal’s initial deflections—or if they ever directly accuse NeBaal of “not being Enge”—NeBaal comes clean.

To handle an early reveal, scan the “[[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|NeBaal’s Confession]]” scene from the end of chapter 4, noting which parts of the conversation make sense for your group’s story so far. Use those to structure your own scene. If NeBaal has bonded with the PCs, emphasize their sorrow over what they saw as a necessary lie, and their relief at being honest with those they view as friends (or even family). If NeBaal hasn’t bonded with the PCs, instead focus on their shared affection for Enge—and their common goal of defeating the Gilded Few.

After the reveal, NeBaal joins the PCs as a companion (see “Companions” in chapter 8 of the *Mistborn Handbook*), but they still avoid the Gilded Few.

#### Broken Trust

If the PCs refuse to work with them, NeBaal asks for a chance to earn the party’s trust, offering to serve the PCs and their personal interests. Deeply conflicted, NeBaal even offers to form a Contract with the PCs—but only as a last resort.

## Adventure Summary

The *Mistborn Legacy* adventure follows the stories of two groups of heroes, split by three hundred years of history. Both parties deal with a powerful Hemalurgic artifact, the [[Items/Legacy Unique Items/Eternity Spike GM ONLY|Eternity Spike]], while opposing a noble supremacist secret society bent on using the Spike for their own ends.

Before starting [[Journal/02 - 01 Echoes of the Lost|chapter 1]], review the Eternity Spike description in [[Journal/13 - Appendix C Eternity Spike|appendix C]], review this adventure summary, then run a session zero using the “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction|Character Connections]]” section at the end of this introduction. This all helps tie the PCs into the plot, making the story feel personal and exciting for the players.

The following sections summarize the adventure background and its nine chapters.

## Adventure Background

The story begins in Era 1. During the Final Empire, a lesser-known obligator named Vadilek Elariel (he/him) became obsessed with creating a heretical utopia, free of the Lord Ruler, Inquisitors, and koloss, ruled purely by the nobility. Rather than seeking leadership for himself, Vadilek aspired to become the “father” of Scadrial’s next ruler, mentoring and ruthlessly training a group of young nobles he dubbed the Gilded Few. Among their ranks numbered Delina Tekiel, Shan Elariel, Idren Seeris, Triss Urbain, Yestal Haught, and Engeanthe Elariel—Vadilek’s niece.

Coveting power and secrets, Vadilek pressured Engeanthe to infiltrate the Canton of Inquisition. Engeanthe refused, rejecting Vadilek and the Gilded Few, so he undertook the infiltration himself, eventually becoming a Steel Inquisitor. Learning of a secret metal—nicrosil—and theoretical Hemalurgic methods, Vadilek dreamed of creating the Eternity Spike: a Hemalurgic spike so incredibly Invested, it could bring about his utopic future by transforming one of his protégés into a divinity. Vadilek created a secret Hemalurgic laboratory in Urteau to develop his Eternity Spike, and he encouraged heightened competition between his students to determine who was worthy of godhood.

When the Lord Ruler was slain, Vadilek enacted his plan. He seized Hemalurgic texts in the downfall and began producing the Eternity Spike. This Hemalurgic process required Vadilek to steal massive amounts of Investiture, so he imprisoned and tortured over a thousand Urteau skaa. In addition, he encouraged Misting nobles in the Gilded Few to sacrifice themselves to create Metalborn-empowered spikes—amassing more power for Vadilek’s noble-born god. As a final act to immortalize his legacy, Vadilek planned to sacrifice himself in order to “donate” his own Inquisitor spikes, completing a latticework to enable the Ascension of his chosen one.

During this time, Vadilek, sensing Ruin’s outside influence, grew increasingly paranoid. This led him to two erratic decisions. First, instead of choosing his favored student—the talented and ruthless Delina Tekiel—he would elevate Engeanthe, his niece who “nobly” refused him. Second, Vadilek made a Contract with the Elariel house kandra, NeBaal, to carry out his will: Find an heir worthy of the Spike, should anything go wrong with Enge’s Ascension.

The Era 1 adventure opens the day of the Ascension ritual—which, indeed, doesn’t go as Vadilek had planned, soon landing the Eternity Spike in the hands of the Era 1 PCs.

### Three Hundred Years Later…

Due to the actions of the Era 1 PCs, the Eternity Spike was secured in a vault, and NeBaal encoded its hidden location in a group of artifacts known as the Gilded Map, scattering the pieces. Now, three hundred years later, the legend of the Eternity Spike is lost—except among the Gilded Few, a noble secret society seeking to reclaim the Eternity Spike, and the Guardians (an organization inspired by the Era 1 PCs) who strive to protect it.

The Gilded Few have also passed down their imperfect knowledge of Hemalurgy and Vadilek’s lattice work as “familial” spikes, including creating new ones. The spikes are kept secret and inherited through the Gilded Few’s noble lines, empowering members through the generations.

As the Era 2 story opens in [[Journal/06 - 05 Engines and Espionage|chapter 5]], a group of three noble prodigies lead the new generation of the Gilded Few, calling themselves the Gilded Trio. Through cunning and power, they’ve discovered the Gilded Map and stand on the cusp of finding and using the Eternity Spike… that is, until the Era 2 PCs stumble onto their plans.

## Notable Characters

Over the course of the adventure, the heroes meet allies and adversaries intertwined with the fabric of Scadrial. The following sections detail the notable named characters in the Era 1 and Era 2 sections of *Mistborn Legacy*.

## Delina Tekiel Era 1

A powerful Mistborn, Delina Tekiel (she/her) is the main antagonist of Era 1. Confident and driven, Delina became the de facto leader of the Gilded Few after Vadilek’s death.

### Delina’s Story

A younger first cousin of the head of House Tekiel, Delina is of high rank but not directly in the line of succession. She was one of Vadilek’s most devoted pupils, vying against Shan Elariel to become his chosen favorite. Delina developed a close bond with Enge, whom she didn’t consider a rival. Delina never knew why Enge left the Few—only that she never said goodbye.

When House Tekiel withdrew from Luthadel, most of Delina’s family fell to House Hasting assassins. Vadilek convinced Delina to stay out of the infighting, and she still feels guilty for not coming to her family’s aid. When Shan was murdered, Delina assumed she’d be chosen to bear the Eternity Spike.

But despite her years of brutal training and sacrifice, Vadilek still passes Delina over, and instead chooses Enge for godhood. Delina becomes enraged. During the Ascension ritual, she executes a bloody coup, killing Enge—deeming her old friend a defector and Vadilek’s unworthy pawn.

Now, Delina will stop at nothing to get the Eternity Spike back from the party, reclaim her right to divinity, and reveal “Enge’s” true identity.

## NeBaal

[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Notable/NeBaal|NeBaal]] (they/them) is a Sixth Generation kandra who allies with the PCs in both eras. They’re willing to sacrifice everything to protect Vadilek’s legacy—the Eternity Spike—and ultimately ask the PCs for the same commitment.

### NeBaal’s Story in Era 1

NeBaal worked for the Elariel family for over two centuries before Vadilek became their main Contract holder. NeBaal always had a soft spot for Enge and felt Vadilek pushed his niece too hard.

Before the Ascension ritual, Vadilek made a Contract to have NeBaal carry out his will if he fell. NeBaal was charged to take the Eternity Spike, keep it safe, and ensure it passed to a worthy noble heir. NeBaal also spied on the party for months before the events of [[Journal/02 - 01 Echoes of the Lost|chapter 1]]. See “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Handling NeBaal|Handling NeBaal]]” earlier in the introduction for critical information on playing this NPC during Era 1.

At the end of [[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|chapter 4]], the Gilded Few capture NeBaal and imprison them in a distinctive silvery urn.

### NeBaal’s Story in Era 2

By the time the Era 2 story begins, the Gilded Few have embedded themselves as an elite fraternity (the Order of a Thousand Eyes) at the University of Elendel, and NeBaal’s yearly interrogation is part of their initiation ceremony.

Staying grounded over three hundred years of imprisonment with their Blessing of Presence, NeBaal has continually refused to reveal the location of the Eternity Spike. Only the Gilded Trio have managed to trick them into parting with any information—and even that was just enough to begin the search for the Gilded Map.

When the Era 2 PCs free NeBaal in [[Journal/07 - 06 Desire of a Hungry Soul|chapter 6]], the kandra has missed three centuries of history and technological advances. Hungry to be free, NeBaal wants the Gilded Few defeated, largely so they can continue their life without fear.

## Bayron Conrad Era 2

[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Notable/Era 2/Bayron Conrad|Bayron Conrad]] (he/him) is an entrepreneur, an inventor, and one of the Gilded Trio, the major antagonists of Era 2. After joining the Trio and discovering Hemalurgy, Bayron gave himself the powers of Pewter Allomancy (Pewterarm) and Steel Allomancy (Coinshot), provided by two steel Hemalurgic spikes in his torso.

### Bayron’s Story

Sterling Conrad, Bayron’s father, built an oil empire in the Northern Roughs. Bayron spent his youth at private, noble-only boarding schools in New Seran, where—being awkward and not Metalborn—he was mocked and dismissed as being from a lesser noble family. While he struggled socially, he showed a penchant for engineering.

Bayron’s first major invention was a more efficient oil pump. When an accident with the pump killed six workers, Sterling shut the project down; Bayron’s shame and the resulting schism with his father hardened into a core belief that all worthwhile progress has a terrible cost. Looking to remake himself, Bayron went to study metallurgy, history, and engineering at the University of Elendel.

There, Bayron found Vadilek’s writings and uncovered the Order of a Thousand Eyes. Obsessed with joining the fraternity, he eventually befriended Lysarra and Kwylliam and discovered Hemalurgy.

Bayron dropped out of university to start Conrad Industries, which would eventually grow to produce weapons, armored vehicles, and communications equipment. Bayron is seen as one of Elendel’s finest: a prodigy, inventor, and thought leader.

## Creating Characters

This section describes how to run a session zero before starting play, where players can collaborate to create their characters, establish who they are in the world, and determine how they know each other. For your *Mistborn Legacy* session zero, help your players create a crew of Era 1 PCs by doing the following:

1.  **Set Expectations.** Run a “Session Zero” as described in chapter 13 in the *Mistborn Handbook*. It offers guidance on using your session zero to establish safety tools, set expectations for the tone of your campaign, and discuss how closely you want to follow canon.

2.  **The Crew.** Use the “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Who Are the Characters?|Who Are the Characters?]]” section to introduce the core concept for the PCs’ crew.

3.  **Legacies.** Use the “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Character Legacies|Character Legacies]]” section to introduce the legacies mechanic, and follow the instructions for picking legacies.

4.  **Make Characters.** Once players choose a legacy and understand the campaign’s premise, it’s time to make 1st-level characters. Full instructions for character creation can be found in chapter 1 of the *Mistborn Handbook*.

5.  **Connections.** Use the “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction|Character Questions]]” section to establish some shared history and connections between crew members.

6.  **Goals.** Finally, the “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Adventure Goals|Adventure Goals]]” section offers advice on picking goals relevant to the adventure.

At the end of [[Journal/05 - 04 Eternitys Vault|chapter 4]], you’ll create Era 2 characters (who start at 3rd level) using the same steps.

## Who Are the Characters?

In each era, the PCs already belong to a “crew” before the adventure begins: a crew of smugglers in Era 1, and of treasure hunters in Era 2. Players must build characters who fit each crew’s concept; rivalries and petty drama can inspire fun roleplay, but the characters generally need to like each other and work well as a team. Share the following descriptions of the adventuring party with your players.

### The Era 1 Party

**Era 1 Crew Goal:** *Succeed as smugglers after the fall of the Final Empire.*

The Era 1 PCs are a small-time smuggling crew, based on a canal boat traveling between Urteau and Luthadel. In a single day, the PCs might first pose as nobles to deliver a shipment of rare books, then hold a meeting with a skaa thieving crew. While the fall of the Final Empire has changed the business, the crew’s smuggling skills are still in high demand.

Enge is the crew’s boisterous boss. She brought them together on the canal boat a few years ago, and she looks out for the party like a big sister. See “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction|Notable Characters]]” earlier in this introduction for more on Enge.

### The Era 2 Party

**Era 2 Crew Goal:** *Uncover historical artifacts to share with the world.*

The Era 2 PCs (who start at 3rd level) are an Elendelbased group of treasure hunters and rogue scholars whose theories (or personalities) make them chafe against polite society. They share a common goal: learning more about the past by “liberating” Luthadel artifacts, revealing important truths about Scadrial’s history.

Professor Azmine Wilko is the party’s closest ally and co-conspirator. Wilko supports the party’s adventures by researching and providing leads. See “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction|Notable Characters]]” earlier in this introduction for more on Wilko.

## Character Legacies

Character **legacies** represent a PC’s history and place in the world. By using legacies, you can weave your players’ original characters directly into the setting and story of Mistborn Legacy via legacy moments—spotlight scenes offering personal choices, revelations, or roleplay opportunities. Legacies also tie the heroes together across the centuries—so each Era 1 PC’s actions impactthe story of a connected Era 2 PC.

### Using Legacies

To use PC legacies, do the following:

**During Era 1 Character Creation.** Have each player select one of the six Era 1 legacies for their PC, answer the legacy’s character questions, and apply the legacy’s expertise (in addition to those gained during character creation).

**During Era 2 Character Creation.** Have each player choose an Era 2 legacy with a connection to one of the legacies players chose in Era 1 (as shown on the PC Legacy Connections table). For example, if your players picked the Official, Convict, Urchin, and Noble legacies for Era 1, then you should only offer the Lawkeeper, Storyteller, Entrepreneur, and Heir legacies for Era 2. Encourage players to pick legacies tied to each others’ characters. Then have them answer the legacy questions and apply the legacy’s starting reward (as determined by the actions of their Era 1 legacy connection).

**During Play.** When you see a “PC Legacy” sidebar, run this as a legacy moment for that specific PC. If you don’t have a character with the specified legacy in play, just skip that sidebar.

## Era 1 Legacies

Have each player select one of these six legacies for their Era 1 character.

### Chronicler Era 1

![[Chronicler.svg]]

The Chronicler has been called to bear witness to unfolding history. Their records will serve as a bastion of truth in the world that is to come. Their task may be a personal one, or it may be driven by an institution of historians, such as the Feruchemist Keepers of Terris.

#### Character Questions

- What is the focus of your historical recordkeeping?

- How do you keep a record? Does your record have a name?

#### Additional Expertise

The Chronicler gains either the Wayfarer or Terris Dominance cultural expertise. This choice might reflect their prior travels or where they’ve gathered their records.

### Convict Era 1

![[Convict.svg]]

The Convict has experienced a stay at the Lord Ruler’s pleasure. Having pulled off a daring jailbreak, they needed vigilance to avoid the Steel Ministry. Now, Enge’s smuggling operation is a natural place for them to make a new life.

#### Character Questions

- What crime were you convicted of, and did you actually commit it?

- How did you break out of prison?

#### Additional Expertise

The Convict gains either the Law Enforcement utility expertise or the Underworld cultural experience, reflecting what they learned during their time in the system.

### Noble Era 1

![[Noble.svg]]

The Noble once occupied a position of privilege in the Final Empire’s cruel hierarchy. Whether through defiance or failure, they’ve been outcast and left to find their own way in an unforgiving world.

#### Character Questions

- What is your family name, and what were they known for?

- Why can you never go back to your noble family?

#### Additional Expertise

The Noble gains either the High Society or Luthadel cultural expertise. The latter is a good fit if they represented their family in the capital political scene.

### Official Era 1

![[Official.svg]]

Every empire needs its bureaucrats. The Official is a former obligator of the Steel Ministry (or they otherwise worked closely with the governance of the Final Empire). After the Lord Ruler’s fall, the Official left their former role to join Enge’s operation. Though they may be haunted by their time with the Ministry, they now have a chance to use their skills somewhere better.

#### Character Questions

- Which canton of the Steel Ministry were you most associated with (such as Finance, Orthodoxy, or Resource)? Do you have an obligator’s eye tattoos?

- What caused you to recognize the weight of the injustices committed by the Ministry or your institution?

#### Additional Expertise

The Official gains either the Steel Ministry utility expertise or the Inner Dominances cultural expertise. This choice might reflect whether they worked more closely with the Ministry or the nobility.

### Urchin Era 1

![[Urchin.svg]]

Despite their humble beginnings, the Urchin is determined to escape their destitute life and rise out of obscurity. It’s an uphill battle, however: While the Lord Ruler is dead, classism is alive and well in Luthadel. Nobles generally respond to the Urchin with suspicion, while skaa are usually sympathetic.

#### Character Questions

- What special thing can you do that made you indispensable to the crew?

- Would you rather join the wealthy elite on top or level the playing field?

#### Additional Expertise

Most Urchins gain the Underworld cultural expertise. Those bent on joining the nobility at the top (or who prefer to work as noble impersonators or con artists) might instead gain the High Society cultural expertise.

### Veteran Era 1

![[Veteran.svg]]

Whether they were pressed into service as a commoner or took a position of leadership on behalf of their noble house, the Veteran fought in the Lord Ruler’s skirmishes. After years of distinguished service, they eventually became dissatisfied or disgusted with the realities of war in the Final Empire. The Veteran then deserted to control their own fate.

## Character Questions

Before the adventure starts, establish some shared history and lore. The crew in each era has been working together for over a year. After many dangerous tasks, they’ve learned to trust and rely on one another. Establish some character connection details by doing the following:

**During Era 1 Character Creation.** Ask each player to answer the “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Enge Connection Questions Era 1|Enge Connection Questions]]” and the “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Era 1 Party Connections Questions Era 1|Era 1 Party Connections Questions.]]”

**During Era 2 Character Creation.** Ask each player to answer the “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Era 2 Party Connections Questions Era 2|Era 2 Party Connections Questions.]]”

### Enge Connection Questions Era 1

Have each player pick one of the following questions and answer it for their character:

1.  Enge is good at fixing things, whether physical or emotional. She recently helped you sew, mend, or repair something important to you. What was it?

2.  Enge trusted you when nobody else did. What had you done that made you seem especially untrustworthy?

3.  On an early smuggling run, Enge encouraged you to do something you didn’t know you’d be good at. Turns out you were. What’s your hidden talent?

4.  You were the first person Enge asked to join her smuggling crew, several years ago. At first, you were reluctant. What did she offer to persuade you to join?

5.  Enge once lost something of hers to keep you safe during a job. What did Enge sacrifice for you?

6.  Enge kept a secret of yours. What was it?

### Era 1 Party Connections Questions Era 1

Have each player pick a question to pose to the party. One other player should provide an answer, creating a connection between their two characters. Every player should ask at least one question, answer at least one question, and collaborate to define and agree on the exact nature of their character’s party connections. Use the following questions or create your own:

1.  I have a friendly competition with you that we trackwith an ongoing tally. What’s the competition? Who’s currently winning?

2.  You and I worked together before joining Enge’s crew. What did we do, and why did we get along so well?

3.  When we do a smuggling job, you and I always impersonate a noble duo. What’s our alter egos’ dynamic—and how is it the exact opposite of our real friendship?

4.  You’ve spent hours teaching me how to do something. What was it, and where on our boat do we train?

5.  I’ve started a small ritual before each job. What is it, and why do you enthusiastically partake?

6.  I feel obligated to keep you safe on smuggling runs. Why?

7.  Early on, when I was ready to leave the crew, you convinced me to stay. What did you promise me?

8.  We share a secret about something that went wrong on a smuggling run. You and I are the only ones who know. What happened?

## Adventure Goals

During character creation, the Mistborn Legacy adventure asks players to answer several questions about their character’s legacy (see “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Character Legacies|Character Legacies]]”) and relationships with other characters (see “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction|Character Questions]]”). Based on their answers to these questions, work with each player to create at least one personal goal for their PC to start with, representing an achievement the PC wants to accomplish, a mystery they wish to solve, a debt they mean to repay, or similar.

Goals related to PC legacies are a strong choice, as legacy sidebars in each chapter present chances to mark milestones (and create new goals). Goals related to locations and NPCs in Luthadel in Era 1 and Elendel in Era 2 are also great choices, as the PCs get a chance to explore these cities during each downtime (see [[Journal/15 - Appendix E Downtime|appendix E]]).

Additionally, the Era 1 PCs have worked with Enge, their smuggling crew boss, for at least a year; as such, PCs could set goals related to traveling with or helping Enge or their crewmates. Goals involving Enge offer intriguing narrative potential, given the secrets that unfold in the story.
