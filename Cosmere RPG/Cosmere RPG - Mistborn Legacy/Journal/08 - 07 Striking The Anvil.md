# 07: Striking The Anvil

## Desire of a Hungry Soul

After escaping the University of Elendel with NeBaal, the party begins their search for the Gilded Map. With the first piece—the Raven Mask—already in hand, the party now turns north. The next map piece lies in the Roughs, held by a fearsome koloss outlaw.

## Running This Chapter

Once the party regroups, NeBaal reveals the next Gilded Map fragment: the [[Items/Legacy Unique Items/Unity Medal|Unity Medal]], awarded after the 1024 FE Workers’ Rights Protest (featured in [[Journal/04 - 03 The Many and the Few|chapter 3]]). The PCs must travel to the Roughs and compete with Bayron Conrad to secure the medal.

## Chapter Flow

A talk with NeBaal illuminates the kandra’s backstory, explains the Gilded Map, and identifi es the Unity Medal as the party’s next goal. Ten days of downtime follow; during that time, either research or a talk with Professor Azmine Wilko points to Weathering. The party’s train north is ambushed by the Ironbrands—a gang of outlaws led by the Anvil (see “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Forging the Anvil|Forging the Anvil]]”). Once the party reaches Weathering, a lawman named Merid offers help. Review appendix F for information on the Guardians secret organization, of which Merid is a member.

The party quickly discovers Bayron Conrad is also after the Unity Medal. Bayron has left operatives and hazards in Weathering to prevent the party from following to the ghost town of Feltrel. He hopes to reach the Anvil there and persuade her to part with the medal before the PCs can interfere. The PCs must explore Weathering and learn about the Anvil’s past and plans while dealing with Bayron’s operatives.

The PCs confront Bayron at the Ironbrands’ hideout, where the party can try to sway the Anvil to their side. It may be helpful to review “Roleplaying Bayron Conrad” in this book’s introduction before running this high-stakes conversation. Once the PCs secure the medal, they must contend with Bayron and his armored car to get out alive.

## Forging the Anvil

The Anvil’s real name is Elanda Wilko. If the PCs gather enough clues (by doing research, talking with Merid, and investigating around Weathering), they can learn the fate of Elanda’s father. He died in a preventable accident, when an “innovative” new oil pump from Conrad Oil—Bayron’s first major engineering accomplishment—malfunctioned. To protect Bayron’s reputation, the Conrads buried the story, and the Wilkos never got justice.

The Anvil’s need for vengeance fueled her rise (first by accepting koloss spikes, then banditry, and finally political freedom-fighting) and eventually drove a wedge between her and the rest of the Wilko clan. Persuasively leveraging this story is one sure way for the PCs to succeed when negotiating with the Anvil at the end of this chapter.

## Character Advancement

The Era 2 characters start this chapter at 5th level and advance to 6th level after the shootout in Feltrel. PCs can complete a “Learn About Hemalurgy” goal (see “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Handling Hemalurgy|Handling Hemalurgy]]” in this book’s introduction) if they obtain Bayron’s Hemalurgy notes.

## Fading Trail

The chapter begins immediately after the events of chapter 6. As the party flees the university with NeBaal, read:

The University of Elendel dissolves into the mists, the hiss of distant rail lines masking your quick footsteps.\
Even exhausted as they are, NeBaal looks around with palpable curiosity. Their gleaming eyes drink in your faces, the towering buildings around you, the paved roads. “Luthadel… has changed,” they rasp. “Is there somewhere safe we can talk?”

## The Whole Truth

If the party seeks safety with Professor Wilko, see “Meeting Wilko” later in this chapter. Otherwise, the party can establish a new hideout (see “[[Journal/07 - 06 Desire of a Hungry Soul#A Long Night|A Long Night]]” in chapter 6).

Once the party is somewhere safe, NeBaal shares what they know, starting with the following:

“I am NeBaal. I claimed to be the Gilded Map, but that’s not quite true. That wretched Order at the University believed I hold the knowledge of where to find a longlost birthright of theirs—but what they see as a treasure, I know to be something extraordinary, absolutely unique, and terribly dangerous.\
“I’m not the Gilded Map—but I am the map’s creator. The ‘treasure’ is actually a centuries-old artifact, and the map is the only way to locate it. Unfortunately, individuals more dangerous than the Order have also started looking for this map… which means I—or perhaps we—need to find it first.”

NeBaal openly answers questions, revealing everything they know (except information about the third map piece; they won’t reveal what or where that might be). They freely offer the following:

**What is the treasure/artifact?** “I once served a noble named Vadilek Elariel. He created an artifact of unspeakable power, forged in innocent blood, believing he could use it to replace the Lord Ruler and shape the future of Scadrial. He called the artifact the Eternity Spike. When his hubris became his downfall, it fell to me to keep it safe. And for centuries, that Spike has remained hidden.”

**What is the Gilded Map?** “The Eternity Spike was hidden beneath Luthadel, in a maze of cavernous tunnels nearly impossible to navigate. I mapped those tunnels, split the map into pieces, and arranged for the pieces to be protected. To keep the knowledge secure, I told the map’s original guardians there were two pieces—but there is actually a third. This combination of three items is what they call the ‘Gilded Map.’ And now I fear it’s falling into exactly the wrong hands.”

**Who else is after the map?** “The Order of a Thousand Eyes is a feeder group for a dangerous secret society called the Gilded Few. The Gilded Few are entitled and largely ineffectual nobles obsessed with obtaining the Eternity Spike. I kept the map secret for centuries. But a few years ago, a trio of students from the Order ‘freed’ me—much like you have done—and tricked me into revealing everything. Only when they imprisoned me again did I realize their true intent was not to help me safeguard the Eternity Spike, but to use it themselves. I hope… I just hope you’re better than they were. For all our sakes.”

**What exactly is the Eternity Spike?** “A spike of rare metal, supercharged by the dark art of Hemalurgy, powered by abhorrent experiments involving the torture of thousands of skaa. Once created, the Eternity Spike had such monstrous power, a single person could not bear it without augmentation. So Vadilek and the Gilded Few killed Mistings, Mistborn, and Feruchemists to create a latticework of spikes, enabling one of their number to bear the Eternity Spike.”

**Where are the map pieces?** “The world has changed. Where the map pieces were hidden may not be where they lie now—but I can track them down. The first is a mask, worn at a ball. The second is a medal, born of a workers’ revolt. I should be able to find records of both. That being said… the last time I thought I’d been granted freedom, it was a mistake for me to reveal all I knew. We’ll speak of the third map piece once we’ve found the first two.”

If the PCs reveal they already have the [[Items/Legacy Unique Items/Raven Mask|Raven Mask]], NeBaal is incredibly relieved and immediately becomes more inclined to trust the PCs. If the PCs don’t mention the mask, NeBaal learns about it from Wilko, and fills the party in as needed. NeBaal plans to research the locations of the mask (if unaware the PCs have it), the [[Items/Legacy Unique Items/Unity Medal|Unity Medal]], and the way to Old Luthadel, inviting the PCs to assist.

## Downtime to Research

After debriefing with NeBaal, the PCs have ten days of downtime to spend assisting NeBaal with research, visiting Wilko, or engaging in other downtime activities (see “[[Journal/15 - Appendix E Downtime|Downtime in Elendel]]” in appendix E).

### Visiting Wilko

If a PC seeks out Wilko, NeBaal requests to join, keen to meet a historian. Refer to “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Meeting Wilko|Meeting Wilko.]]”

### Researching

The National Archives and the university library are logical locations to research, but if the PCs triggered the Archives Lockdown event or ran afoul of campus security, they must disguise themselves with a successful DC 15 Thievery test to research at that corresponding location.

Conducting research requires ten full days of downtime and 3 boxings per day in supplies. Ask whether each participating PC is researching the medal or the location of Old Luthadel, then have them make a DC 14 test using Deduction, Lore, or Persuasion. Depending on the result, they learn the following about their chosen topic:

**Unity Medal.** Succeed or fail, the party’s research reveals the medal was lost after the Catacendre but has recently surfaced again in Weathering. A success on the test also reveals information about Bayron Conrad: A rare broadsheet clipping reports Bayron’s genius innovations started in the Northern Roughs, where he invented a pump for his father’s oil operation, Conrad Oil. It was hailed as revolutionary, but strangely, the accomplishment is never mentioned again.

![[pg134_MBL07_Chasing History_David Alunan.webp]]

**Old Luthadel.** On a success, the party’s research reveals the location of Old Luthadel, the buried capital of the Final Empire. Additionally, it reveals information about Lysarra Tekiel: She’s said to be building a secret rail line with no known destination. Rumor also has it the Roughs is experiencing grain shortages and diverted supplies. Taken together, this suggests Lysarra already knows the location of Old Luthadel and may be moving large amounts of resources there.

### Failing to Research

If the party fails to research successfully, NeBaal still discovers the location of Old Luthadel, and Wilko always knows the medal is in Weathering. However, if the entire party chooses not to research, during this downtime, PCs notice they’re being tailed by one of Conrad’s operatives (**[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Conrad Tineye Informant Era 2|Conrad Tineye informant]]**). Later, the operative in “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Train to Weathering|Train to Weathering]]” has bodyguards.

### Hidden History

A month ago, the Anvil picked up the Unity Medal during a Tekiel train heist, stashed in a crate of other loot. She guessed the medal is valuable, but has no idea about its history—and she’s never heard of the Gilded Map.

## Meeting Wilko

Wilko can be found lying low with her family in the Clan Estates. If the PCs don’t visit before then, she tracks down the party on the 10th day of downtime. NeBaal is interested in meeting Wilko, asking to join the PCs for this conversation. Wilko brings up the following:

**Wounded Wilko.** If Wilko was unconscious after the bombing, she’s still recovering from her injuries. She asks, “Is someone trying to kill us?” and wants to know what became of the Founder’s Key.

**Healthy Wilko.** If Wilko was conscious, she instead asks what happened during the Trials. If NeBaal is present, she’s shocked and delighted to meet a Faceless Immortal. Between her kindness and the party’s obvious trust in her, NeBaal quickly warms up to her.

**Unity Medal.** Wilko can explain off handedly that the Unity Medal was created in 1024 FE, and can quickly help pinpoint its current location in Weathering.

**Raven Mask.** Any mention of the mask or the Gilded Map quickly leads NeBaal to learn the party already has Cadme’s masquerade mask. If NeBaal hasn’t yet explained the significance of the mask—the first piece of the Gilded Map—they do so now.

**To Weathering.** Wilko recommends traveling to Weathering by Tekiel train and seeking **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Named/Ch. 7/Merid|Merid]]** (he/him), a Terris lawkeeper. Wilko hands over a wooden token they can show Merid as proof of identity (see “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#What Wilko Knows|What Wilko Knows]]”). She requests they keep it safely hidden.

Wilko is eager to anonymously record the party’s adventures, is fascinated by NeBaal, and is highly invested in the party obtaining the Gilded Map. Before the PCs leave, she has two requests:

**Wilko’s Family.** Wilko says she has family in the Roughs, and she asks the PCs to keep an eye out for her cousin **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Named/Ch. 7/Elanda The Anvil Wilko|Elanda]]** (she/her). “Growing up, she was like a sister, but I haven’t seen her since childhood. Last I heard, she was working as a lawkeeper—that was a while back, around when my uncle died. If you happen to run into her… tell her I miss her, I’m sorry, and to come visit.” Wilko hasn’t talked to Elanda in years and has never heard of the Anvil (see “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Forging the Anvil|Forging the Anvil]]” at the start of this chapter).

**NeBaal’s Company.** Wilko would love NeBaal to stay with her while the party heads to the Roughs; NeBaal accepts unless the PCs request otherwise.

### What Wilko Knows

If questioned about the token she gives them, Wilko attempts to cover—poorly—before quickly caving and confessing she’s a junior member of a secret society called the Guardians of Industry/Wisdom/Labor (the focus of the group is determined by the Era 1 party’s choices, as described in [[Journal/16 - Appendix F The Guardians|appendix F]]). She explains the token signifies that the Guardians consider the PCs trustworthy. She promises Merid can explain more.

## Tricks on the Tracks

Train tickets to Weathering are easy to acquire. The journey takes about a day and a half. If Wilko was conscious after the bombing, she secures the party a private compartment; otherwise, they can pay 100 boxings for one.

## Train to Weathering

The party departs from a Tekiel trainyard in the Eighth Octant. If they investigate the station beforehand, consult “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Rumors on the Train|Rumors on the Train.]]” When the PCs are ready to go, read:

Steam hisses as a train rolls into the station, then passengers jostle out onto the crowded platform like ants swarming a picnic.\
Dabbing sweat from his brow, the station manager glances at a group of uniformed company officers. They all bear the Tekiel crest, armed and alert.\
The conductor hollers, “All aboard!” The Tekiel officers march forward, a trickle of other passengers pressing in behind them.

The train includes the following cars (listed from front to back), depicted on map 7.1:

- T1: Freight Car

- T2: Dining Car

- T3: Passenger Car

- T4: Caboose

The party’s compartment is in the passenger car (T3). An operative of Bayron’s sits in the next adjacent compartment, tracking the party. Tekiel officers (use the **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Lawkeeper Era 2|lawkeeper]]** stat block) patrol through every car.

### Rumors on the Train

While traveling, the party can freely explore the dining car (T2) and passenger car (T3), gathering intel through conversation or eavesdropping.

Each PC who succeeds on a DC 12 Persuasion or Perception test can roll on the [[Journal/17 - Appendix G Tables|Rumors on the Train]] table. On a failure or Complication, a PC must succeed on a DC 15 Persuasion test to convince Tekiel officers they aren’t outlaws, or else the Tekiels become suspicious (which matters during “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Battle on Board|Battle on Board]]”). After three failures, the PCs are detained in the caboose (T4).

Not all rumors are true, as indicated in parentheses after each rumor.

### Bayron’s Operative

In the passenger car (T3), **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Named/Ch. 7/Helina Dobs|Helina Dobs]]** (Conrad Tineye informant, she/her) sits in the compartment next to the PCs, eavesdropping.

If the PCs skipped researching during downtime, Helina has been surveilling them for some time now, and two **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Bodyguard Era 2|bodyguards]]** accompany her to keep intruders out of her compartment. Fighting makes the Tekiel officers suspicious.

Helina surrenders immediately if confronted alone. A successful DC 14 Persuasion or Intimidation test convinces her to turn over her notebook and admit she works for Conrad Industries. If she surveilled the PCs during downtime, her notes also describe each PC’s downtime activities.

## Battle on Board

After the PCs gather intel, a fancy meal is served in the dining car—included free with their train tickets. Ask which PCs attend dinner, and establish where any other PCs are located on the train (see map 7.1).

The Ironbrands attack during the meal, targeting the freight car (T1) for food and supplies. PCs in the dining car (T2) witness the ambush. Others can hear the commotion from any car. To begin the fight, read:

In the dining car, the clink of forks and porcelain cups is drowned by the train’s rumbling. “Excuse me,” a man says, hailing a patrolling Tekiel officer. She marches over to him.\
An ear-splitting boom shocks the room into silence. The Tekiel officer collapses, blood pooling around her unmoving body. The man shoots up from his seat and throws off his cloak, revealing a shotgun and leather duster. An outlaw. Footsteps thud on the train roof overhead, audible throughout every train car.\
“Hands up!” the outlaw snarls, grabbing a hostage— a noble in a fancy striped New Seran suit. “Everybody stay polite, and ain’t nobody else gotta die.”

As this combat begins, three Ironbrands (two **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Freedom Fighter Era 2|freedom fighters]]** and one **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Enforcer Era 2|enforcer]]**) draw weapons in the dining car (T2) and take a hostage (**[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Commoner Era 2|commoner]]**). Two more Ironbrands (**[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Freedom Fighter Era 2|freedom fighters]]**) land on the freight car (T1) roof, plant charges, and retreat to the dining car roof until the charges explode.

Each round, the Ironbrands try to advance their plan:

**Round One.** The Ironbrands on the rooftop plant explosives on the freight car, while the Ironbrands in the dining car create a diversion. The footsteps on the roof are audible from any car.

**Round Two.** The explosives in the freight car detonate. The Ironbrands in the dining car continue fighting. The Ironbrands on the rooftops move to subdue the Tekiel officers in the freight car.

**Round Three.** The Ironbrands in the freight car seize the supplies. If the hostage hasn’t been rescued, the Ironbrands in the dining car attempt to shoot him in the leg before releasing him. Finally, all the surviving Ironbrands leap from the train.

### Battlefield Effects

During the combat, the following effects are active:

**Blocked Doors.** The three Ironbrands in the dining car (T2) block the path to the freight car (T1). Until these Ironbrands are defeated, PCs can only bypass them via the roof.

**Moving Train.** When a character ends their turn on the roof, they must succeed on a DC 12 Agility or Athletics test or fall Prone. Ensuing failures also push them 15 feet toward the back end of the train.

**Tekiel Officers.** Tekiel officers assist the PCs in the freight car (unless the party is under suspicion). Each round, one enemy takes 2d6 keen damage as the officers open fire.

#### [[Journal/17 - Appendix G Tables|Battle on Board Opportunities and Complications]]

### Aftermath

The encounter ends when the Ironbrands are subdued or their plan is complete. If the Ironbrands are stopped, the freight is secured. Assuming the PCs contributed, Tekiel officers tell the party to visit them at the Tekiel freight office in Weathering (see area [[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#W2: Tekiel Freight Office|W2]] on map 7.2) for a 250-boxing reward. If the Ironbrands aren’t stopped, they escape with the supplies.

Either way, the PCs spot the Anvil as combat ends. Read:

In the distance, a lone rider observes the train, reins in hand. A koloss woman, broad and motionless as a mountain. After a moment, she spurs her massive horse into a gallop, vanishing into the horizon. The train rattles on.

After the raid, Tekiel officers question witnesses while other passengers retreat to their compartments. The PCs can interrogate the Ironbrands or search their bodies to learn:

- The bandits are called the Ironbrands, and their leader is known as the Anvil.

- The Ironbrands are stealing food because folks in the Roughs are starving.

- The Ironbrands blame the Tekiels for the supply shortages.

## A Seed Planted

The train arrives just after noon. Read:

The scorching sun is high in the sky as the train screeches to a halt in Weathering. You’re greeted by wind-scarred buildings and two waiting lawkeepers.\
One, thickly built with a hard stare, strides toward the conductor. The other, a tall Terris man in an olive duster, ambles toward you.\
“Name’s Merid,” he says, tipping his hat and flashing a genial smile. “Barl’s gettin’ the story from the crew.” He leans in. “Azmine told me you were coming. Let’s step somewhere quiet.”

Merid ushers the PCs away from the crowded train platform then requests proof of the party’s identity.

If the PCs do confirm their identities, Merid offers assistance, advice, and intel. Continue to “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#In Good With the Guardians|In Good With the Guardians.]]”

If the PCs don’t provide the token from Azmine, he refuses to help. Without Merid’s assistance, the PCs must investigate Weathering on their own, and it costs 5 boxings per PC to rent horses to get to Feltrel; if this happens, Merid secretly tails the PCs there, reappearing with the Guardians to assist in the climactic battle. ## In Good With the Guardians

Once the PCs confirm their identities to Merid, read:

“From Azmine’s description, it sounds like you kicked up more dust since that Archives heist than most folks do in a lifetime,” Merid says approvingly. “I’m impressed, and so are the folks I work with. I understand you’re after the Unity Medal, tryin’ to put a certain map together…?” He gives you a long look, and then breaks into a grin.\
“I think we can help each other out. The good news is, I know where the medal is: in the hands of an outlaw by name of the Anvil. The bad news is, the Gilded Few are plottin’ something. A bunch of city-slickers with fancy gadgets came in on the earlier train.”

Merid readily answers questions on topics including:

**Azmine Wilko.** “We go way back. Azmine stayed out here for a few years when we were kids, before she got all professorial. She’s good people, and I told the folks I work with as much.”

**The Anvil.** “The Anvil’s the leader of the Ironbrands—big city types call them outlaws, but they’re mostly stealin’ to feed folks, what with the supply shortages. I’d leave her be, but she’s got the Unity Medal… and I think she’s planning something big. Maybe dangerous.”

**Elanda Wilko.** When talking about Professor Wilko or the Anvil, the PCs can make a DC 12 Insight test. On a success or if they ask about Azmine’s cousin, Elanda Wilko, they sense Merid’s reluctance. If pressed, his ever-present smile dims and he shares: “Elanda Wilko is the Anvil. It’s a long story, and it ain’t much fun. We rode together as lawkeepers once. Round when her dad died, she left the ’keepers. Wanted me to leave, too. I was just starting up with the Guardians, so I told her no—and she got real mad, said I cared more about protecting dusty old relics than people. She became full koloss, changed her name, and won’t talk with me. I didn’t want to trouble Azmine, and it’s… complicated, in a way city folk don’t understand.”

**The Guardians or the Gilded Map.** “I’m with the Guardians. For now, let’s just say we’re a quiet group that likes to keep dangerous things outta dangerous hands. And that makes us real interested in helpin’ you keep the Map away from the Gilded.”

**The Gilded Few or the Gilded Trio.** “Guardians’ve been tangling with their kind for too long, if you ask me. The Gilded always have some big plan, monologuing about their grand legacies and whatnot, but it always just boils down to nobles thinkin’ they can take whatever—or whoever—they please. Conrad’s even been nosin’ around here, trying to bribe the lawkeepers with fancy weapons. But we ain’t for sale.”

Merid won’t tell the PCs much more about the Guardians until they’ve proven themselves.

### Merid’s Proposition

Once questions are answered, Merid has a proposition for the PCs. He explains:

“I’m hopin’ you can persuade the Anvil to give up the Unity Medal—without anybody killing or arresting her, seeing as she’s keepin’ folks in the Roughs fed.\
“Investigation is the key. While I muster some horses and backup, you poke around town. If you can find out where she is, what she cares about, and what she’s plannin’, I reckon you can win her over, no problem.\
“The more you find out, the better. I worry she’s got more than train jobs in mind.”

If the PCs agree to investigate, Merid promises to contact his Guardian allies and secure horses for the party—transportation to wherever the Anvil’s hideout is located. He says he’ll be back around dusk and points out the areas of interest in Weathering (see map 7.2), suggesting the PCs start investigating in Barrowcrest, on the outskirts of town.

## Exploring Weathering

The party is free to explore Weathering and gather information about the Anvil, but they must deal with dangerous operatives Bayron left behind.

## Hot on the Anvil’s Trail

The Anvil’s location (an abandoned Survivorist chapel in the ghost town of Feltrel) is easy to uncover. More investigation is required to piece together the Anvil’s history and her current plans, as follows:

**History.** The Anvil was first a lawkeeper, then a criminal, and now a freedom-fighter—a chain of events sparked by her father’s death, for which she blames Bayron Conrad. (See “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Forging the Anvil|Forging the Anvil]]” in this chapter’s introduction.)

**Plans.** The Anvil’s currently trying to gather the recruits, resources, and dynamite necessary to blow up a noble mining operation. Her aim is to establish an independent Roughs nation, cutting Elendel nobles out of oil fields and mines, building railways, and forming a trade authority. What she does, she does for the people of the Roughs—and she prefers to do so violently.

## Timed Trouble

### Facing Vereta

Run the match as a special conversation (see “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Resolving the Conversation|Resolving the Conversation]]”). Only one PC can engage Vereta in the ring at any given time. Every round, each PC makes a single contribution, the type of which depends on whether they’re in the ring with Vereta or in the crowd.

### In the Ring

The PC in the ring engages Vereta directly. Whenever this PC fails a test, they lose 2 focus. On a success, Vereta spends focus to resist influence, believing herself to be the better fighter still. Possible contributions include:

**Trade Blows.** The PC shows Vereta their prowess in the ring, making an Athletics or Agility test against Vereta’s Physical defense (19) to fight her or dodge her swings.

**Boast.** The PC wins Vereta’s respect by boasting of their heroic deeds. This test is made against her corresponding defense (DC 14) and gains a disadvantage if the crowd is against the PCs (due to either failed crowd work or the Conrad operative).

### In the Crowd

PCs who aren’t in the ring can’t engage Vereta directly, but they can influence the crowd. Possible contributions include:

**Work the Crowd.** A PC might win the crowd over with a catchy chant (DC 15 Leadership) or by speaking to smaller groups (DC 16 Persuasion or Intimidation). On a success, the crowd favors the PCs, and Vereta resists influence by spending 1 focus. On a failure, the crowd turns against the PCs.

**Tag In.** A PC can tag into the ring by making a DC 10 Agility or Leadership test. Regardless of the outcome, they swap with the PC in the ring, but on a failure, they also lose 1 focus.

**Resist Bayron’s Interference.** A Conrad operative lurks among the crowd, spreading rumors the PCs are Elendel spies, turning the crowd against the party. A PC can identify the saboteur with a successful DC 13 Perception test, and silence her with a successful DC 14 Persuasion or Intimidation test. If silenced, she reveals Bayron hired her to delay the party and find dirt on the Anvil, so that Bayron can claim the Unity Medal. Unless this operative is dealt with, the conversation can’t fully succeed (see “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Resolving the Conversation|Resolving the Conversation]]”).

### Resisting Influence

Vereta has a Cognitive defense of 14, a Spiritual defense of 14, a Physical defense of 19, and 5 focus. She admires bold strength and glory-filled stories.

### Resolving the Conversation

The conversation ends in total success if Vereta no longer has enough focus to resist influence—so long as Bayron’s operative was dealt with (otherwise, it’s only a partial success). The conversation fails if the PC currently in the ring runs completely out of focus. If the operative wasn’t defeated, she slips into the crowd and disappears.

**Total Success.** The crowd cheers; Vereta is happy to talk. If asked about the Anvil, she shakes her head and describes Elanda as a “stubborn young’un and frustrating idealist.” When Elanda’s father died, Elanda demanded vengeance against the oil company, even though everybody in town agreed it was just an accident, including the local press. But Elanda was eaten up over it, eventually leaving both her family and Weathering behind to form the Ironbrands. Vereta mentions that **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Named/Ch. 7/Derruk|Derruk]]** at the church ([[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#W6: Church of the Survivor|W6]]) knew her well, but warns he’s superstitious.

**Partial Success.** Vereta will still talk about the Anvil, but only in exchange for 100 boxings to buy food and medical supplies for the clan.

**Failure.** The crowd jeers; Vereta dismisses the PCs.

### Special Rule: Conrad Cuts In

Bayron brought a suitcase full of boxings, promises to provide weapons, and a desperate desire to win at all costs. After each PC contribution, Bayron cuts in with an attempt to undermine their arguments or outbid them: PC contributions gain a disadvantage until Bayron is removed from the conversation.

Bayron’s position is precarious. The Anvil blames Conrad Oil for her father’s death; she’d sooner kill a Conrad than take his money. Once Bayron is removed from the conversation, PC contributions no longer gain a disadvantage, but the Anvil must still be persuaded to hand over the Unity Medal.

### Beginning the Negotiation

If the PCs foiled the train raid, the Anvil starts the conversation by asking the PCs to make amends for the lost food and supplies. A PC can mollify her with a gift worth at least 500 boxings, or explain they didn’t understand her righteous goals at the time, gaining a disadvantage on the test. If a PC fails a test, the Anvil grows more suspicious of them, and the next PC contribution gains a disadvantage. If a PC gives a gift or succeeds on a test, the Anvil is mollified and spends 1 focus to resist, conceding that what’s done is done.

After that, the PCs might discuss topics like the following:

**Bribes and Barter.** The Anvil seeks resources to further her cause, and she’s receptive to offers of money, weapons, or new recruits. If a PC fails a test, the Anvil implies they don’t have deep enough pockets for her plans. If a PC succeeds, she spends focus to resist, bargaining hard for more.

**A Revolutionary Purpose.** If the PCs ask about Jaja’s dynamite or succeed on a test to determine what the Anvil wants, she shares her plan to blow up a noble mining operation to progress her vision for Roughs independence. Voicing support for this goal can win her over, but if a PC fails their test, the Anvil sighs and dismisses the party as idealists without teeth. If a PC succeeds, the Anvil spends focus to resist, remaining hard to impress: “Easy words to say. Harder to live by.”

**Mentioning Azmine.** If the PCs mention Professor Wilko, the Anvil freezes in surprise, then asks how her cousin is doing. She’s curious, but bitter. She only has to spend 1 focus to resist arguments that claim Azmine wants her to give over the medal, dismissing her cousin as a “soft cityfolk academic” who doesn’t understand “the real world.”

**Mentioning the Anvil’s Father.** If the PCs mention the Anvil’s father, she immediately loses 1 focus, regardless of the test result, launching into an angry rant about the abuses and exploitations of the nobility. The Anvil doesn’t resist successful arguments that helping the PCs will hurt those responsible for her father’s death. This is a good place to raise the stakes.

### Resisting Influence

**The Anvil** has a Cognitive defense of 15, a Spiritual defense of 15, and 5 focus. She finds arguments extremely strong if they appeal to her hatred of nobles or promise to provide weapons, but she only has to spend 1 focus to resist arguments claiming problems can be solved without violence.

### Resolving the Conversation

The conversation ends successfully when the PCs make especially compelling arguments or when the Anvil no longer has enough focus to resist influence. If the PCs threaten the Anvil or if the party fails multiple attempts to sway her, the conversation ends in failure.

**Success.** The Anvil agrees to give the Unity Medal to the PCs. If Bayron wasn’t removed from the conversation, his face turns blotchy red as he seethes, “Nobody bests a Conrad!” and stomps out the chapel doors. Either way, Bayron is waiting outside the chapel with his armored car, and he attacks the PCs as soon as they emerge; proceed to “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Shootout at Sundown|Shootout at Sundown.]]”

**Failure.** The Anvil refuses to give the Unity Medal to the PCs. If Bayron wasn’t removed from the conversation, she gives him the medal in exchange for the suitcase of boxings, and Bayron attempts to escape in his armored car. Otherwise, the Anvil plans to keep the medal, and the PCs must leave empty-handed or face the extremely dangerous prospect of fighting the Anvil and six Ironbrands (five freedom fighters and one koloss clan protector) to take it.

If the PCs fight the Anvil, Bayron flees and is long gone by the time they leave the chapel; proceed directly to “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Aftermath|Aftermath.]]”

## Shootout at Sundown

Bayron’s forces include **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Notable/Era 2/Bayron Conrad|Bayron Conrad]]**, two **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Conrad Operative Era 2|Conrad operatives]]** (one wielding a flamethrower, detailed in [[Journal/12 - Appendix B Items|appendix B]]), two **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Garrisoner Era 2|garrisoners]]**, and two **[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Commoner Era 2|commoners]]** driving an armored car (see [[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#Battlefield Effects|Battlefield Conditions]]). When Bayron reaches 15 health or less, he attempts to retreat to his armored car, unwilling to die for the medal (see “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#A Killer Deal|A Killer Deal]]”). The PCs then have one round to destroy the car before it speeds away to Elendel. If Bayron is defeated, any surviving operatives pile into the armored car and flee.

### Battlefield Effects

The following effects are active during the combat:

**Too Young (and Rich) to Die.** When Bayron is reduced to 0 health, he remains conscious and tries to surrender. He dies if he takes any further damage. Proceed immediately to “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#A Killer Deal|A Killer Deal.]]”

**Armored Car.** Two of Bayron’s crew (**[[Actors/Legacy Adversaries/Various/Era 2 Adversaries/Commoner Era 2|commoners]]**) sit within in his armored car, one as the driver and the other as a passenger. The driver spends 3 each slow turn to operate the vehicle, moving it up to 80 feet. The car’s access hatch is locked, but it can be opened with a successful DC 22 Athletics test. A character can also attack the car directly: It has a Physical defense of 18, 60 health, and a deflect value of 5. When the car has 30 health remaining, the hatch breaks open.

**Rotary Gun.** The armored car is fitted with a mounted ***rotary gun*** (see chapter 7 of the *Mistborn Handbook*), which is usable by the armored car’s passenger. The passenger uses their turn to Strike with the rotary gun as many times as possible, making a Heavy Weaponry attack test (+0 bonus) and rolling 3d4 keen damage.

## A Killer Deal

If the PCs reduce Bayron to 0 health, he drops to his knees, throws up his hands, and barters for mercy:

“Wait! I’ll give you 5,000 boxings if you just let me leave!” Any remaining operatives similarly surrender.

Bayron is willing to do anything to survive, begging for his life with tears in his eyes; if a PC attempts to discern his truthfulness, they sense that he intends to make good on his promise to pay. Bayron also offers his Hemalurgy notes if pressed (see “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Handling Hemalurgy|Handling Hemalurgy]]” in this book’s introduction), promising power and revealing the two railway-like spikes through his torso.

If the PCs agree to let him go, Bayron surrenders the ***[[Items/Legacy Unique Items/Unity Medal|Unity Medal]]*** if he has it, cuts the PCs a check they can cash at the Tekiel freight office in Weathering, gathers any surviving lackeys, and speeds away in his motorcar. The check is valid and can be cashed without incident; Bayron’s operatives have withdrawn from town.

## Aftermath

If Bayron falls, and if he had the Unity Medal, the PCs can collect it. Additionally, if a PC has the Hemalurgist expertise, they can attempt to remove Bayron’s two steel ***Hemalurgic spikes*** from his corpse; one grants Pewter Allomancy, and the other grants Steel Allomancy (see “[[Journal/01 - 00 Introduction#Handling Hemalurgy|Handling Hemalurgy]]” in this book’s introduction). If all operatives are defeated, the PCs gain a ***[[Items/Legacy Unique Items/Flamethrower|flamethrower]]*** (detailed in [[Journal/12 - Appendix B Items|appendix B]]). If the PCs defeat the Anvil, and she previously gave Bayron the medal, the PCs can also collect Bayron’s briefcase containing 1,500 boxings.

After the battle, the Guardian forces disappear back into the desert, and Merid approaches the PCs. If she survived, the Anvil returns to her chapel hideout to plan her next move. When the party is ready to continue, proceed to “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil#United|United.]]”

## United

If the PCs secured the medal without fighting the Anvil or taking Bayron’s deal, Merid is overjoyed. If the PCs fought the Anvil, Merid is visibly disappointed to hear it, taking a sorrowful moment to process the bad news. Either way, so long as the Unity Medal is retrieved and the PCs didn’t take Bayron’s deal, Merid’s mission is accomplished: Increase the Guardians organization scale by 1. Merid congratulates the PCs, promises an introduction to the Guardians, and finally reveals the secret organization’s full purpose (see the “[[Journal/08 - 07 Striking The Anvil|Time Skip Connection]]” sidebar and [[Journal/16 - Appendix F The Guardians|appendix F]]). He offers the PCs the chance to join and gain access to the organization’s resources.

If the PCs failed to secure the medal (but didn’t take Bayron’s deal), Merid is only momentarily disappointed before visibly bouncing back. Read:

“Well, the good news is, there’s three map pieces,” he says, tone optimistic. “The fewer pieces the Gilded get their pasty paws on, the better. We’re not beat ’til we quit."

The Guardian’s scale doesn’t increase, but Merid still invites the PCs to join the Guardians. Otherwise, if the PCs took Bayron’s deal, read:

“Letting that one go might come back to bite you.” Merid pauses. Though bloodied, he cracks a smile. “But I’m sure y’all will bite back."

The Guardians scale doesn’t increase, and Merid doesn’t offer the PCs a chance to join the Guardians.

“Folks like the Gilded Few pose a mighty threat.” Merid looks out across the battlefield. The sun surrenders to the horizon, and the veil of purple night drapes over Feltrel. “But this is how we beat them,” he says, tipping his hat to you. “Together.”
