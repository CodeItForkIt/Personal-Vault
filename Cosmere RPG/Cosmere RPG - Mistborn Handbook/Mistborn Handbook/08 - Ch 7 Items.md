# Ch. 7: Items

## Items

Adventuring through the mist- and intrigue-steeped world of Scadrial can be harrowing, and your party will need gear to survive and thrive. In addition to boxings (Northern Scadrial’s currency), stocking up on weapons, armor, and equipment will aid you in journeying through the world.

For long or distant expeditions, you may also want to know about travel costs, including lodging, mounts, and vehicles. And if your character wants bespoke gear of their own design, they can even engage in some crafting.

## Using Items

Most rules for using items are presented in their specific sections, but here are a few general rules that apply to multiple kinds of items.

## Expert Traits

Unless otherwise specified, you can use the items in this chapter without any special expertise. However, some weapons and armor have **expert traits**, giving benefits that only apply if you have an expertise in that weapon or armor.

## Charges

Some items (such as a Feruchemist’s metalminds) have one or more **charges** that power certain effects. For Feruchemy, a metalmind’s maximum charges are either determined by your ranks in Feruchemy, or—for unkeyed metalminds and Feruchemical medallions (see [[Mistborn Handbook/09 - Ch 8 Goals and Rewards|chapter 8]])—by when that item was created. Charges typically represent how much of the Feruchemy power’s related trait can be stored within its metalmind.

Individual items may contain more information on how to use their charges, but the following rules apply to all of them, unless otherwise specified.

### Activating Items With Charges

Some items with charges must be activated in a special way (such as by using the [[Actions/Basic/Interact|Interact]] action). If not specified, you can spend a charge without spending an action. When you spend a charge, you trigger that item’s effect, then the charge is lost until you recharge that item in the specified manner.

### Recharging Items

An item with charges specifies how it can be recharged. If no method is listed, that item’s charges can’t be regained.

## Invested Items

Some items are permanently infused with Investiture, connected to Metalborn powers, or otherwise connected to the Cognitive Realm or Spiritual Realm. These items are **Invested** and can be more difficult to affect—and in some cases, detect—with Allomancy and other effects.

Hemalurgic spikes and items infused with any form of Investiture are Invested, as are Feruchemical metalminds with 1 charge or more.

If an effect works differently on Invested items, this is specified in the effect’s rules.

## Items With Statistics

In this handbook, the names of items are usually listed without special formatting. However, when it might be helpful to know that a mentioned item has specific rules or statistics, the item name appears in bold italics. For example, a written adventure might state that “the characters find a *[[Items/Equipment/Lockpick|lockpick]]*”; this lets you know that this isn’t just a narrative item, and you should check this chapter to find out how it works in the rules.

## Currency

In both of Scadrial’s eras, coins serve as the primary currency in most regions. For simplicity, this book tracks money using only one type of coin, **boxings** (sometimes abbreviated as “bx”). If you ever receive money in other denominations, the GM will simply tell you its value in boxings. However, Scadrians commonly use three denominations:

**[[Items/Loot/Clip|Clip.]]** Clips are small coins made of common metals, such as iron, tin, and bronze. A single clip is worth 0.01 bx. These also come in denominations of 5-, 10-, 20-, and 50-clip coins.

**[[Items/Loot/Boxing|Boxing.]]** Imperial coins, colloquially called boxings, are larger—about twice the size of clips—and typically made of more precious metals like gold. A single boxing is, of course, worth 1 bx. Before the Catacendre, there were 2- and 5-boxing coins, and more denominations were introduced in Era 2.

**[[Items/Loot/Note|Note.]]** Paper notes exist in both eras, though during Era 1, these bills are generally found only in noble hands. A 1-note bill is worth 10 bx. 5-, 10-, 20-, and 50-note bills are also circulated.

These denominations bear different likenesses depending on the era. In Era 1, they depict the Lord Ruler and his palace, Kredik Shaw. In Era 2, they depict legendary heroes of the Final Empire—such as the Ascendant Warrior, the Hero of Ages, the Lord Mistborn, and other members of the Survivor’s crew— in addition to prominent landmarks such as Elendel.

The prices in this book apply equally to both eras unless otherwise specified.

## Starting Kits

During step 6 of character creation, you choose one of the following sets of equipment. Each **starting kit** grants items such as boxings (roll the dice indicated to determine how many boxings you start with), weapons or armor (see the Weapons tables and the Armor table later in this chapter), other equipment (see the Equipment table later in this chapter), and more.

## Weapons

This section outlines the most common **weapons** found on Scadrial. The Weapons tables in this section include the following columns:

**Type.** The general type of weapon—but feel free to creatively describe your own, as long as it uses the same statistics.

**Skill.** The skill you test when attacking with this weapon. (All weapons on the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Light Weaponry]] and [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Heavy Weaponry]] tables use the corresponding skill.)

**Damage.** How much damage the weapon deals when it hits or grazes.

**Range.** How far away the weapon can attack (see the upcoming “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Weapon Range|Weapon Range]]” section).

**Traits.** Special rules that apply to that weapon (see the upcoming “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Weapon Traits|Weapon Traits]]” section).

**Expert Traits.** Special rules that apply only if you have an expertise in that weapon. Some weapons remove an undesirable trait if you have expertise, rather than adding a desirable trait; for example, if you have the Longswords expertise, your longsword loses its Two Handed trait so you can wield it with one hand.

**Weight.** The weapon’s weight.

**Price.** The weapon’s standard price.

See the “[[Mistborn Handbook/11 - Ch 10 Combat|Attacking]]” section of chapter 10 for the full rules about attacking using these weapons.

## Wielding Weapons

Each weapon requires one hand to **wield** unless otherwise specified in its traits. While wielding a weapon, you can use it to attack or use other abilities. Rules that apply to “your weapon” only apply if you’re wielding that weapon.

If you aren’t wielding a weapon (or holding an object) in a hand, you have that **hand free** for other abilities that require it. If both of your hands are full but an ability doesn’t state you need a hand free, it’s up to the GM to decide whether you could reasonably use that ability in the narrative.

### Drawing Weapons

You can use the [[Actions/Basic/Interact|Interact]] action (1) to draw a weapon in your possession and grasp it in your hand. After you do, you’re wielding that weapon and can use it for attacks and other effects.

You can use the [[Actions/Basic/Drop|Drop]] free action (0) to release a weapon you’re holding (for example, right before you Interact to draw a different weapon). When a scene begins and you’re not [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Surprised|Surprised]], it’s assumed you can instinctively draw your weapons before the first round (without using the Interact action). If you’re [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Surprised|Surprised]] when a scene begins, you can’t instinctively draw your weapons.

### Offhand Weapons

While wielding more than one weapon, you must choose which one is in your **main hand**. Attacks with that weapon are made as usual.

When you wield a weapon that’s not in your main hand, it’s in your **offhand**. Before you use the Strike action using a weapon in your offhand, you must spend 2 focus. (Attacks other than the Strike action don’t require spending that focus.)

Some characters might wield their weapons using methods other than hands. You can do this if you’re only wielding weapons that another character could with two hands. When you do, pick which weapon is considered your “main hand” and “offhand” (regardless of how you hold them), and otherwise follow the weapon rules. Unless your GM decides otherwise, you can’t wield more than two weapons (or one Two-Handed weapon) at a time, regardless of whether you use your hands to do so.

## Ammunition

If your weapon has the Loaded or Ranged trait, you don’t need to purchase its ammunition or track it on your character sheet (unless the weapon uses special ammunition). However, the Loaded trait does limit how many shots you can make with a weapon before reloading. It’s assumed you can always obtain or improvise standard ammunition for your weapons, unless the GM decides you’re in an unusual situation where ammunition is unavailable.

## Weapon Range

Weapons can only attack targets within a certain range. Each weapon is classified as either a melee weapon or a ranged weapon. See “[[Mistborn Handbook/11 - Ch 10 Combat|Targeting and Range]]” in chapter 10 for more information on range and reach.

### Melee Weapons

You can use a **melee weapon** you’re wielding to attack a target within your reach. When wielded, some melee weapons increase your reach for attacks made with that weapon. On the Weapons tables, if a melee weapon’s range lists a number in brackets, increase your reach by that many feet. For example, if you usually have a 5-foot reach, a “Melee [+5]” weapon extends your reach to 10 feet.

### Ranged Weapons

You can fire ammunition from a **ranged weapon** you’re wielding, attacking a target either within or beyond your reach. The weapon’s range lists two numbers in brackets, such as “Ranged [80/320]”; the first number is the weapon’s short range in feet, and the second is the weapon’s long range in feet. As described in “[[Mistborn Handbook/11 - Ch 10 Combat|Targeting and Range]]” in chapter 10, ranged attacks outside short range gain a disadvantage.

Additionally, as described in “[[Mistborn Handbook/11 - Ch 10 Combat|Attacking]]” in chapter 10, you gain a disadvantage on ranged attacks while within an enemy’s reach. If you attack a target within 5 feet of your ally, raise the stakes; a Complication risks grazing your ally.

## Weapon Traits

These rules apply to any weapon with that trait listed in its entry on the Weapons tables:

**Blast [X].** The number in brackets expresses the weapon’s blast radius in feet. After you hit or graze a target with this weapon, you also graze every other character within that radius. On a hit, you can spend O from the attack test to instead hit every grazed character.

**Cumbersome [X].** To wield this weapon easily, your Strength score must be equal to or greater than the number indicated in brackets. If your Strength is lower than that number, you gain a disadvantage on all attacks using this weapon and are [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Slowed|Slowed]] while wielding it.

**Dangerous.** The GM can spend C from an attack with this weapon to cause you to also accidentally graze an ally within the weapon’s reach or range. This deals the usual damage for a graze.

**Deadly.** When you hit a target with this weapon, you can spend O to cause the target to immediately suffer an injury.

**Defensive.** While wielding this weapon, you can use the [[Actions/Basic/Brace|Brace]] action without cover nearby.

**Discreet.** This weapon is less obtrusive than others, and thus less likely to be confiscated in secure settings. In non-combat scenes, you gain an advantage on any test you make to disguise this weapon, to hide it on your person, or to convince others not to take it from you.

**Fragile.** When you attack with this weapon, the GM can spend C to cause it to break after the attack is resolved.

**Indirect.** This ranged weapon can arc shots over cover and obscuring terrain. If a target isn’t in your line of effect but you can sense them, you can still attack them with this weapon if there’s a reasonably open path for your projectile to indirectly arc to them. Your target can’t benefit from the [[Actions/Basic/Brace|Brace]] action against attacks made with this weapon.

**Loaded [X].** This weapon stores ammunition equal to the number indicated in brackets. To make a ranged attack with this weapon, you must spend 1 stored ammunition. As 1, you can reload this weapon to full ammunition. The GM can spend C from an attack with this weapon to reduce your stored ammunition; after they do, the weapon only has only one shot remaining.

**Momentum.** When you attack with this weapon, if you already moved at least 10 feet in a straight line toward your target on this turn, you gain an advantage on the attack.

**Offhand.** While wielding this weapon in your offhand, it only costs you 1 focus (instead of 2) to Strike with it. Pierce. This weapon’s damage can’t be reduced by the target’s deflect value.

**Quickdraw.** You can use the [[Actions/Basic/Interact|Interact]] action as 0 to draw this weapon.

**Single-Use.** After you use this weapon, it is permanently expended.

**Thrown [X/Y].** You can throw this weapon at a target, making a ranged attack when you do (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/11 - Ch 10 Combat#Ranged Attacks|Ranged Attacks]]” in chapter 10). The two numbers in brackets express the weapon’s short and long range; as with ranged weapons, you gain a disadvantage when attacking a target outside short range. Once the weapon is thrown, it is lost until you recover it from your target.

**Two-Handed.** You must wield this weapon in two hands, not just one. When you attack with it, it uses both hands.

**Unique.** This weapon has unique rules. These are detailed either in the weapon description or immediately following the word “Unique” in the Weapons tables (for example, “Unique: loses Two Handed trait”).

## Improvised Weapons

In the heat of the moment, you might wish to attack with an object that isn’t a traditional weapon (such as hitting someone with a pot) or use a weapon in an unconventional way (such as swinging a rifle at someone like a staff). If you can pick an object up in one or both hands, you can use it as an improvised weapon!

**Expertise.** Unlike other entries on the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Special Weapons]] table, you can choose Improvised Weapons as a weapon expertise. If you do, your improvised attacks benefit from the expert traits of the weapon most similar to it, even if you don’t have expertise in that similar weapon.

**Using an Improvised Weapon.** When you attack with an improvised weapon, the GM decides which non special light or heavy weapon it is most like. Make the attack as though with that weapon, but add the Fragile trait (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Weapon Traits|Weapon Traits]]”).

## Unarmed Attacks

Punches, kicks, thrown elbows, headbutts, and bites are examples of unarmed attacks. You can make an **unarmed attack** using Athletics.

**Expertise.** Unlike other entries on the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Special Weapons]] table, you can choose Unarmed Attacks as a weapon expertise.

**Unique Traits.** Unarmed attacks add the following unique traits:

**Always Available.** Unarmed attacks don’t count as weapon attacks, but they’re melee attacks intrinsic to each character. You don’t have to be holding a weapon to make an unarmed attack, and you can’t be disarmed of your unarmed attacks. You can make an unarmed attack even if both of your hands are holding weapons or other items, but when you do, that attack still counts as an attack using a hand of your choice. If you use the Strike action to make an unarmed attack, you might describe it as a punch, kick, or headbutt—but you must still choose which “hand” is making that attack, and you can’t make another Strike using a weapon you’re holding in that hand.

**Strength Training.** When you make an unarmed attack, its damage die is based on your Strength score, as shown on the Unarmed Damage table. Many Scadrians use knucklebones (presented in “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items|Equipment]]”) to heighten the damage of their unarmed attacks.

### Dueling Canes

A favorite of hazekillers, [[Items/Weapons/Dueling Cane|dueling canes]] are wooden canes crafted to withstand combat despite appearing as a standard walking cane. Their weight and size are precisely designed to accommodate a wide variety of fighting styles.

**Unique Trait.** Dueling canes have the following unique trait:

**Flexible Fighting.** You can use either the Heavy Weaponry or Light Weaponry skill with a dueling cane.

### Koloss Blades

[[Items/Weapons/Koloss Blade|Koloss blades]] are massive and outrageously heavy chunks of metal—usually iron—roughly smelted in the shape of a sword. They’re nearly as long as the koloss who wield them are tall—usually 7 to 8 feet—and about as wide as most humans are broad. After generations of being wielded with brute strength to literally crush opponents, these blades are typically blunted, chipped, and dented, often with jagged edges.

**Unique Trait.** Koloss blades have the following unique trait:

**Unwieldy.** Due to its awkward construction, attacks with the koloss blade can’t graze.

### Soundsticks

[[Items/Weapons/Soundstick|Soundsticks]] are wooden sticks designed to produce a loud and jarring noise when struck; they’re especially effective when banged against another soundstick. Often utilized by hazekillers, these weapons are particularly effective at overloading the tin-enhanced senses of Tineyes and Windwhisperers.

**Unique Traits.** Soundsticks have the following unique trait:

**Ear-Splitting.** While you wield this weapon, you can use 1 to make a Light Weaponry test, comparing the result to the Spiritual defense of each character (other than yourself) whose senses range includes your space. In addition to potentially overstimulating characters with supernatural hearing, each character you succeed against takes 1d6 vital damage and becomes Disoriented until the end of their next turn. If you make this test while wielding two soundsticks, you gain an advantage against characters with supernatural hearing, and on a success against such a character, they also become Stunned until the end of their next turn.

## Firearms (Era 2)

Following the Catacendre, technology on Scadrial improved by leaps and bounds. This included the technology of warfare, giving rise to a variety of combustion-triggered munitions. The development of **firearms** fundamentally altered the nature of combat on Scadrial.

The firearms presented on the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Weapons: Firearms]] table follow the same rules as other weapons. However, firearms are inherently volatile and more intricate than other Scadrian weapons. The [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Firearm Opportunities and Complications]] table provides inspiration for tests using these weapons.

### Dynamite

[[Items/Weapons/Firearms/Dynamite 1 stick|Dynamite]] is a powerful and volatile explosive that’s typically used for demolition and strip mining. It can be used as a weapon, but use great care—it’s incredibly dangerous to even carry in your possessions. Dynamite is typically detonated with a fuse, though it can be thrown in a pinch. Multiple sticks of dynamite can be attached to the same fuse to increase the power of a timed explosion.

Especially in urban population centers, law enforcement officers (such as the constabulary) keep a close watch for hazardous items like this. Unless you have a patron (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/09 - Ch 8 Goals and Rewards|Reward Categories]]” in chapter 8) who authorizes you to acquire, transport, or wield dynamite, it’s incredibly difficult to purchase—and if caught with it, you’re liable to be arrested and prosecuted.

**Unique Traits.** Dynamite has the following unique traits:

**Delayed Detonation.** While touching one or more sticks of dynamite, you can use 2 to attach and light a fuse, choosing a detonation time between 10 seconds and 60 seconds (1 to 6 rounds of combat). After that time, the sticks of dynamite explode. When they do, make a Crafting attack against the Physical defense of each character within 5 feet of the exploding dynamite; see the rules for the Blast trait to determine how you affect targets beyond 5 feet. At the GM’s discretion, you might also damage or destroy objects in the blast radius.

**Volatile.** Though dynamite is safer to transport than pure black powder, dynamite can still spontaneously ignite when exposed to significant shock or friction. When thrown at a character, object, or surface, the dynamite automatically explodes on impact, as if detonated by a fuse (see “Delayed Detonation”). Additionally, while you’re carrying dynamite, the GM can spend C from any of your tests to cause one of your sticks of dynamite to explode, expending that stick and causing it to graze each character (including you) within its Blast range; however, if you have the Dynamite weapon expertise, you can safely carry dynamite without risking this explosion.

### Rotary Gun

A [[Items/Weapons/Firearms/Rotary Gun|rotary gun]] has an array of barrels set on a rotating circular base, typically connected to an ammunition magazine or belt. By manually cranking a lever, rounds are fed into the barrels and fired at an incredible rate.

Especially in urban population centers, law enforcement officers (such as the constabulary) keep a close watch for hazardous items like this. Unless you have a patron (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/09 - Ch 8 Goals and Rewards|Reward Categories]]” in chapter 8) who authorizes you to acquire, transport, or wield a rotary gun, it’s incredibly difficult to purchase—and if caught with it, you’re liable to be arrested and prosecuted.

**Unique Traits.** Rotary guns have the following unique traits:

**Fire Velocity.** You aren’t limited in the number of Strike actions you can make with a rotary gun on your turn. However, each time you attack using a rotary gun, the test gains a number of disadvantages equal to the number of previous attacks you made with it on that turn. For example, when you make your third rotary gun attack in a single turn, your attack test gains two disadvantages.

**Unwieldy Weight.** Due to its sheer weight and recoil, it’s incredibly difficult to use a rotary gun without anchoring it on a stable surface. While the rotary gun isn’t anchored, it has the Cumbersome [6] trait. You or another character who can reach it can use the [[Actions/Basic/Interact|Interact]] action to anchor the rotary gun on a horizontal stable surface; each time you do this, the value of the Cumbersome trait decreases by 2. For example, if you Interact in this way twice, the rotary gun becomes Cumbersome [2]. If an anchored rotary gun is moved from its surface for any reason, it is unanchored and again becomes Cumbersome [6].

## Armor

Across both eras of Scadrial, **armor** is quite uncommon among the citizenry, and it’s especially frowned on in polite society. While wearing armor, you usually stand out from others. Depending on the circumstances, if you don’t have the proper credentials (or forged ones), you might stick out like a sore thumb, be turned away from certain locations, or gain a disadvantage on tests made to influence civilians in contexts such as banquets, markets, or churches.

This section outlines the most common types of armor found on Scadrial. You can only benefit from one type of armor at once, and you can’t put on armor or take it off while in combat or in situations where it’s hard to move. The [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Armor]] table includes the following columns:

**Type.** The general type of armor—but feel free to creatively describe your own, as long as it uses the same statistics.

**Deflect Value.** Reduce all incoming impact, keen, and energy damage by the deflect value of the armor you’re wearing (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/04 - Ch 3 Character Statistics|Deflect]]” in chapter 3).

**Traits.** Special rules that apply to that armor (see the upcoming “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Armor Traits|Armor Traits]]” section).

**Expert Traits.** Special rules that apply only if you have an expertise in that armor. Some armors remove an undesirable trait if you have expertise, rather than adding a desirable trait; for example, if you have the Wooden Half-Plate expertise, that armor loses its Fragile trait for you.

**Weight.** The armor’s weight.

**Price.** The armor’s standard price.

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Armor]]

## Armor Traits

These rules apply to any armor with that trait listed in its entry on the Armor table.

**Cumbersome [X].** To wear this armor easily, your Strength score must be equal to or greater than the number indicated in brackets. If your Strength is lower than that number, you’re [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Slowed|Slowed]] while wearing this armor and you gain a disadvantage on all tests that use your Speed attribute.

**Fragile.** When you’re hit by an attack, the GM can spend O to cause your armor to break after the attack is resolved.

**Presentable.** This unobtrusive armor is presentable to wear in public in a non-martial context. You don’t suffer undue attention for wearing this armor or gain a disadvantage on tests for doing so in conversations.

**Unique.** This armor has unique rules detailed immediately following the word “Unique” in the Armor table (for example, “Unique: loses Fragile trait”).

## Mistcloaks

[[Items/Armor/Mistcloak|Mistcloaks]] are cloaks worn by the rare and powerful Mistborn—Allomancers who can burn every Allomantic metal. More common in Era 1 than in Era 2, these dark cloaks are typically hooded and made of thin strips of lightweight cloth connected to the hood at the wearer’s shoulders. The movement of the long tassels mimics the swirling motions of the mists, making the wearer much harder to spot in obscured environments. Each mistcloak is specially commissioned for its wearer, so different cloaks can range widely in appearance.

**Unique Traits.** Mistcloaks add the following unique trait:

**Flowing Tassels.** While wearing this armor in an area that obscures vision, such as in darkness or mist, you gain an advantage on Stealth tests you make against characters whose primary sense is obscured by that area. Additionally, those characters gain a disadvantage on Perception tests against you.

If you have the Mistcloak specialist expertise, also add the following special expert trait:

**Mistborn Gravitas.** While wearing this armor, you gain an advantage on Intimidation tests against non-Mistborn characters who can sense you and who have no reason to believe you’re not Mistborn.

## Mistcoats Era 2

An homage to the mistcloaks of the Final Empire, the [[Items/Armor/Mistcoat Era 2|mistcoats]] of Era 2 are ankle-length coats that divide into tassel-like strips just above the waist. Like mistcloaks, each mistcoat is specially commissioned for its wearer. They’re designed to be simultaneously protective and fashionable, and a well-worn mistcoat can even mimic some of the effects of the legendary mistcloaks.

**Unique Traits.** If you have the Mistcoat specialist expertise, add the following special expert trait:

**Flowing Tassels.** While wearing this armor in an area that obscures vision, such as in darkness or mist, you gain an advantage on Stealth tests you make against characters whose primary sense is obscured by that area. Additionally, those characters gain a disadvantage on Perception tests against you.

## Metal Vials

Due to the wide use of Allomancy, there is a robust trade of metals in both eras of Scadrial. Metals for Allomancy need to be specifically refined for consumption, as an Allomancer who consumes unpurified metal risks injury.

The eight common metals (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/07 - Ch 6 Metallic Arts|Metals of Scadrial]]" in chapter 6)are cheap. In most circumstances, Allomancers can easily obtain vials with any mix of the eight common metals at no cost (see “Managing Metals” in the “[[Mistborn Handbook/06 - Ch 5 Metalborn Paths#Allomancy: Using Investiture|Allomancy: Using Investiture]]” section of chapter 5). The other metals, however, are much rarer and more expensive, as shown on the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Rare Metal Vials]] table. These vials must purchased individually, so Metalborn who need rare metals usually seek a reliable, recurring supply (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/09 - Ch 8 Goals and Rewards#Rare Metals|Rare Metals]]” in chapter 8).

Each vial contains shavings and beads of the metal, suspended in alcohol or oil for easy swallowing. During a short or long rest, vials can be mixed or separated to produce any combination of rare metals. It’s assumed any vial of rare metals already contains whichever common metals the Allomancer desires (no action required), unless the story calls for a more limited supply.

## Equipment

This section presents everyday Scadrian tools and resources that are commonly applicable to your adventures. The Equipment table lists each item’s name, weight, and price, then the text that follows describes each item’s benefits.

Some items in this section list a range of prices, as many variations on that item can be found across Scadrial. For example, alcohol ranges dramatically in value, and some reference books are much rarer than others. The GM chooses how much an individual item costs within its range, and they might grant tangential benefits to the higher-priced varieties. For example, spending extra money on a top-shelf whiskey, rather than an alley-brewed ale, might lower your DC on a test made to influence a connoisseur.

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Equipment]]

**[[Items/Equipment/Alcohol 1 serving|Alcohol.]]** The cultures of Scadrial have a variety of intoxicating traditions, from the most watered-down ales and beers to the finest whiskeys and wines.

**A bottle of alcoholic spirits - ANNA VERHOOG**

**[[Items/Equipment/Anesthetic 5 doses|Anesthetic.]]** Usually made with ether, you can apply a dose of anesthetic ointment to an injury after a short or long rest to reduce its remaining recovery time by 1d4 days. Each injury can be treated with anesthetic only once per day.

**[[Items/Equipment/Antiseptic potent 5 doses|Antiseptic (potent).]]** Strong antiseptics such as iodine can be extracted from seaweed ash with acid. A dose can be applied to a character after a short rest to restore 2d6 health.

**[[Items/Equipment/Antiseptic weak 5 doses|Antiseptic (weak).]]** A dose of weak antiseptic, such as one made with mercury, can be applied to a character after a short rest to restore 1d6 health.

**[[Items/Equipment/Book reference|Book (reference).]]** Each reference book contains information on a particular topic, such as a historical era, engineering, biology, or architecture. When you have time to reference a book while you make a related test using the Intellect attribute, you’re considered to have an expertise in the topic (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/04 - Ch 3 Character Statistics#Expertise Benefits|Expertise Benefits]]” in chapter 3).

**[[Items/Equipment/Candle|Candle.]]** While lit, the candle sheds light in a 15-foot radius. It burns for up to 6 hours before becoming unusable.

**[[Items/Equipment/Case leather|Case (leather).]]** A sealable, cylindrical case that can be used to store up to ten pages of paper or parchment and protect them from the weather.

**[[Items/Equipment/Chain thick 10 feet|Chain (thick).]]** This sturdy chain can be used in securing cargo, shackling prisoners, and other situations where strength is essential. This chain can be broken by succeeding on a DC 30 Athletics test or by dealing 15 damage to it.

**[[Items/Equipment/Chain thin 1 foot|Chain (thin).]]** This thin chain is often used in jewelry, usually made of precious metals in fine links. This chain can be broken by succeeding on a DC 20 Athletics test or by dealing 5 damage to it.

**[[Items/Equipment/Clothing breakaway|Clothing (breakaway).]]** Breakaway clothes mimic the appearance of fine clothing, but they’re designed to be worn over armor and weapons to disguise them. They can be removed as if a fight breaks out, tearing at predesigned seams without ruining the garment. While wearing an outfit of breakaway clothes, any armor you’re wearing gains the Presentable trait, and each weapon you’re carrying gains the Discreet trait; however, you gain a disadvantage on any attack you make, and each attack targeting you gains an advantage.

**[[Items/Equipment/Clothing common|Clothing (common).]]** Plain clothes are made of materials used by the working class and generally left undyed, helping you fit in among the commonfolk. While wearing an outfit of common clothing, you gain an advantage on spiritual tests to convince others you’re from the working class.

**[[Items/Equipment/Clothing fine|Clothing (fine).]]** Fine clothes are made of silks or other high-quality materials, colored using expensive dyes, and often stitched with fine embroidery. Wearing fine clothes helps you blend into high society, such as noble banquets, without arousing suspicion. While wearing an outfit of fine clothing, you gain an advantage on spiritual tests made to convince others you’re from the upper class.

**[[Items/Equipment/Clothing ragged|Clothing (ragged).]]** This clothing may have once been of fine or common make, but it’s now barely holding together. While wearing these clothes, you gain an advantage on spiritual tests made to convince others you’re destitute.

**[[Items/Equipment/Crowbar|Crowbar.]]** You can use a crowbar to gain an advantage on Athletics tests where the crowbar’s leverage can be applied.

**[[Items/Equipment/Ear Trumpet|Ear Trumpet.]]** Distant sounds heard through an ear trumpet can be sensed as though at half the distance. At the GM’s discretion, more powerful ear trumpets may be available at higher prices.

**[[Items/Equipment/Flint and Steel|Flint and Steel.]]** A flint and steel can be used as 1 to light abundant fuel within your reach. Starting a fire under more challenging circumstances can take 1 minute or more.

**[[Items/Equipment/Food ration 1 day|Food (ration).]]** Rations are foods created for extended travel, such as jerky and hard-baked bread. They generally lack flavor, but if kept dry, they remain edible indefinitely.

**[[Items/Equipment/Food street 1 day|Food (street).]]** In every dominance in Era 1 and in the various cities of Era 2, you can enjoy regional street confections, from common loaves of bread to root vegetable hash and farlet soup.

**[[Items/Equipment/Food fine 1 day|Food (fine).]]** Fine food can be found in high-class restaurants and on the tables of nobles’ feasts.

**[[Items/Equipment/Freechair Era 2|Freechair.]]** (Era 2) Adapted from airship technology, a freechair is a mobility device that levitates and moves using an iron medallion and a Steelpushing primer cube. The operator of a freechair has the same movement rate as other characters (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/04 - Ch 3 Character Statistics#Speed|Speed]]” in chapter 3), and the freechair doesn’t require an action to activate.

Though freechairs are available for purchase, disabled characters can obtain one for free. In Era 1, freechair technology is unknown, but characters can use a wheelchair with the same movement rate.

**[[Items/Equipment/Grappling Hook|Grappling Hook.]]** A grappling hook is typically attached to the end of a rope to aid in scaling or descending from walls and cliffs. A securely anchored grappling hook grants an advantage on Agility and Athletics tests made to climb using its attached rope.

You can use an Interact action to attach the grappling hook to an anchor within your reach without making a test. Alternatively, you can throw and attach it to a suitable anchor within 20 feet of you by succeeding on an Agility or Athletics test (DC set by the GM)

**[[Items/Equipment/Knucklebones|Knucklebones.]]** Street toughs and enforcers in Scadrial’s big cities love using knucklebones. Brass knuckles are common, but when fighting Allomancers, the more expensive wooden variety is worth every coin.

You can wear knucklebones on your hand instead of wielding a weapon in that hand. When you make an unarmed attack using that hand, increase the size of each unarmed damage die by one size (see the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Unarmed Damage]] table earlier in this chapter). For example, damage of 1 (no die roll) becomes 1d4, 1d8 becomes 1d10, and so on.

**[[Items/Equipment/Lantern oil|Lantern (oil).]]** While lit, this lantern sheds light in a 30-foot radius. It burns for up to 6 hours per pint of oil.

**[[Items/Equipment/Lock and Key|Lock and Key.]]** This sturdy lock can be opened with its included key or a successful DC 20 Thievery test. At the GM’s discretion, locks that require higher DCs may be available at higher prices.

**[[Items/Equipment/Lockpick|Lockpick.]]** You can use a lockpick to gain an advantage on Thievery tests made to pick a lock. After this test is resolved, the GM can spend C to cause the lockpick to break.

**[[Items/Equipment/Magnifying Lens|Magnifying Lens.]]** You can use a magnifying lens to gain an advantage on any skill test you make to appraise or inspect a small or highly detailed item.

**[[Items/Equipment/Manacles|Manacles.]]** These metal restraints, which come with a key, can bind the wrists or ankles of a Large or smaller character. They can be escaped with a successful DC 25 Agility test, broken by succeeding on a DC 25 Athletics test or by dealing 15 damage to them, or lockpicked by succeeding on a DC 20 Thievery test. At the GM’s discretion, manacles that require higher DCs may be available at higher prices.

**[[Items/Equipment/Metal Sand pouch|Metal Sand (pouch).]]** You can scatter these fine flakes of scrap metal to confuse a character (such as an Iron Allomancer or Steel Allomancer) who can sense the presence of metals. You can use 1 to throw this pouch to a space within 30 feet of you, breaking the pouch and dispersing its contents. The metal sand creates a cloud in a 10-foot square that remains until the end of your next turn. This cloud obscures both sight and an Allomancer’s ability to sense metal by burning iron or steel. Additionally, Allomancy tests made for [[Metallic Arts/Iron/Iron Allomancy|Iron Allomancy]] or [[Metallic Arts/Steel/Steel Allomancy|Steel Allomancy]] gain a disadvantage if this cloud is in their line of effect.

**[[Items/Equipment/Musical Instrument|Musical Instrument.]]** Instruments are often used for enjoyment, passing time, or busking for clips. When playing an instrument, you can determine the quality of your performance by making a test using a related skill (such as Agility for a stringed instrument, Athletics for drums, or Discipline for an instrument with many keys). If you have an expertise in that instrument, you gain an advantage on the test.

**[[Items/Equipment/Net hunting|Net (hunting).]]** This net of thick rope can ensnare animals or enemies. While holding this net, you can use 1 to make an Athletics test against the Physical defense of a Large or smaller character within 15 feet of you. On a success, the character is [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Restrained|Restrained]] and knocked [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Prone|Prone]]. That character or another who can reach them can use the [[Actions/Basic/Interact|Interact]] action to make a DC 15 Athletics test, freeing the trapped character on a success. If a character is [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Restrained|Restrained]] by multiple nets, they must be freed from each separately.

**[[Items/Equipment/Net fishing|Net (fishing).]]** This net of fine mesh is used to ensnare fish. You can use this net to gain an advantage on physical tests related to fishing.

**[[Items/Equipment/Oil 1 flask|Oil.]]** Oil generally comes in a 1-pint ceramic flask. Oil is flammable and can be used to fuel oil lanterns, to create dangerous terrain in a space, or as an improvised weapon (sling). When you hit or graze a target with this improvised weapon, you coat the target in oil in addition to the normal damage. After the attack, regardless of the result, the ceramic flask breaks and any unused oil is lost.

**Poison.** Often manufactured using gurwraith or the bark of climbing vines like birchbane, poison comes in single-dose glass vials. As 1, you can pour it into food, liquid, or a container, or you can apply the poison to a melee weapon or a piece of ammunition you’re holding. When you do, the GM may require you to succeed on a Stealth test to avoid discovery. When a character ingests the poison or is hit by an attack using the poisoned weapon or ammunition, the poison is expended, and the character suffers the following effects:

**[[Items/Equipment/Poison weak 1 dose|Weak Poison.]]** The target must succeed on a DC 12 Athletics test or take 1d6 vital damage.

**[[Items/Equipment/Poison effectual 1 dose|Effectual Poison.]]** The target must succeed on a DC 14 Athletics test or take 2d8 vital damage and be [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Stunned|Stunned]] for 1 hour.

**[[Items/Equipment/Poison potent 1 dose|Potent Poison.]]** The target must succeed on a DC 16 Athletics test or take 3d10 vital damage and be [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Stunned|Stunned]] for 1 hour. While Stunned in this way, the target is [[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring#Immobilized|Immobilized]].

**[[Items/Equipment/Pulley System|Pulley System.]]** Most commonly used by engineers and construction workers, this system contains four pulley wheels, 30 feet of rope, and a hook. When you have time to set up a pulley apparatus while attempting to lift an object, you treat the object as being a quarter of its normal weight.

**[[Items/Equipment/Rope 50 feet|Rope.]]** Generally made of hemp fibers, rope is generally sold in 50-foot lengths that can be cut and tied to change the rope’s length as needed. It can be cut by dealing 2 damage directly to it, or it can be ripped apart with a DC 20 Athletics test.

**[[Items/Equipment/Scale|Scale.]]** Most commonly used by scholars, appraisers, and gem cutters, a scale can be used to assess the exact weight of an object (to a maximum of 2 pounds).

**[[Items/Equipment/Spyglass|Spyglass.]]** Distant objects viewed through a spyglass can be sensed as though at half the distance. At the GM’s discretion, spyglasses that allow distant objects to appear even closer may be available at higher prices.

**[[Items/Equipment/Surgical Supplies|Surgical Supplies.]]** This satchel contains enough bandages, salves, splints, and other medical supplies to be used 10 times. When you make a Medicine test to treat a wounded character (or to similarly use these medical supplies), you can expend one of these uses to gain an advantage on the test.

**[[Items/Equipment/Tonic 1 dose|Tonic.]]** A pungent tincture formulated of secret ingredients. In Era 1, many nobles enjoy a daily spoonful of Pewterheart Tonic, and in Era 2, countless happy customers swear by the efficacy of Sparkle Tonic. (Meanwhile, others swear it’s just the placebo effect.) Either way, a character can consume a dose after a short rest to recover 1d4 health and 1 focus.

**[[Items/Equipment/Treatment medical 1 dose|Treatment (medical).]]** Scadrian doctors treat ailments with a variety of remedies, generally made from botanicals such as garlic, parsley, willow, and cypress. ## Travel

Whether your adventuring party is a task force deputized by the constabulary, a band of mercenaries who sell your services in the Outer Dominances Era 1 or  the Roughs Era 2, an expeditionary group seeking lost knowledge, or something else entirely, you’re likely to trek far and wide across Scadrial. This section contains information about lodging, mounts, and vehicles that may be helpful when you’re far from home.

## Lodging

While traveling, you may find yourself wanting a room, especially during particularly bad ashfalls. From seedy taverns to the finest of accommodations, the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Lodging]] table lists how much it generally costs to stay per night. You can usually purchase food at or near these lodgings, using the prices on the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Equipment]] table in the previous section.

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Lodging]]

## Mounts

Mounts aren’t particularly common on Scadrial in either era, but horses are available for rent or purchase for the few who can afford them. In both eras, horses are generally owned by nobles and other prosperous, high-class individuals.

The [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Mounts]] table lists the horse’s travel speed and carrying capacity (representing the pace and weight the horse could comfortably maintain during a long expedition).

When attached to a cart, carriage, or other vehicle, a mount can move five times its carrying capacity (in cluding the weight of the vehicle). If multiple mounts are pulling the same vehicle, combine their carrying capacities.

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Mounts]]

## Vehicles

Vehicles enable travel over water or allow quicker or safer travel over land—and in Era 2, they even offer travel through the sky.

The [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Vehicles]] table lists common forms of transportation, including the type of vehicle, average travel speed, and cost to rent or purchase one. The rental rate grants you temporary use of the vehicle and provides you the animals or people needed to operate it. If you’d like to purchase a vehicle, you’ll need to staff it yourself.

## Crafting

Experienced crafters can craft non-Invested items for themselves, including weapons, armor, ammunition, equipment, and even grander creations like buildings or works of art.

![[pg273_HB07_Making a Metalmind_Darko Stojanovic.webp]]

**DARKO STOJANOVIC**

## Crafting Prerequisites

You can only craft an item if you have an expertise relevant to the item you want to craft. For example, to craft a revolver, you need either the Munitions Crafting expertise or the Revolver expertise.

If you only have a broad expertise (like Munitions Crafting), you can craft relevant items with it, but you can’t personally benefit from its expert traits in combat.

## Crafting Mundane Items

If you have the necessary expertise, choose a non Invested item with a price from the Weapons tables, the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Armor]] table, or the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Equipment]] table. At the GM’s discretion, you can craft a different non-Invested item instead. Use the following steps to craft your chosen item:

1.  Acquire the necessary materials.

2.  Make a Crafting test.

3.  Apply any upgrades or drawbacks.

### Step 1: Acquire Materials

For non-Invested items, it’s assumed you can easily find the metal, leather, wood, or other raw materials to craft the item, provided you have access to a common trade network or other market.

**Tools and Facilities.** You need access to the proper tools and facilities to craft the item. This is up to the GM’s discretion based on the specifics of the item you’re making. You might be able to fashion a quick lockpick with just your hands, but a sharp sword requires access to a forge and anvil. If your tools and facilities are poor, you might gain a disadvantage on your Crafting test in [[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Step 2: Make a Crafting Test|step 2]].

**Material Cost.** The cost to acquire the necessary raw materials equals half the normal value of the item you’re crafting.

**Rare Metals.** Depending on the era, some metals are unavailable, and others are rare and expensive to source. When crafting an item that requires a metal from the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Rare Metal Crafting Costs]] table, add the listed cost to the standard material cost to craft an item. Depending on the size of the item you’re crafting, at the GM’s discretion, this price might increase or decrease.

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Rare Metal Crafting Costs]]

### Step 2: Make a Crafting Test

Once you have all needed materials, it’s time to craft the item during downtime (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/10 - Ch 9 Adventuring|Downtime]]” in chapter 9).

**Time Spent.** When crafting, you can usually produce 50 boxings’ worth of work in one workday. For example, a fragmentation grenade worth 75 boxings requires 1.5 workdays to complete, while a lockpick worth 2 boxings requires only a fraction of a workday. However, this time can be modified by Opportunities or Complications during the crafting process: Each Opportunity you roll during your Crafting test reduces the time to create the item by 25 percent, while each Complication increases it by 25 percent.

**Crafting Test.** When you’re ready to begin crafting, roll a Crafting test and raise the stakes. At the GM’s discretion, you might gain an advantage on this test if you have exceptional tools or facilities available, or you might gain a disadvantage if your tools or facilities are exceptionally poor.

Consult the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Item Crafting Test]] table to determine how your test result affects your crafting. And if you roll an Opportunity or Complication, don’t forget to modify your crafting time as described in “Time Spent.”

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Item Crafting Test]]

### Step 3: Apply Upgrades and Drawbacks

For each upgrade you earned as part of your Crafting test, choose an upgrade from the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Item Upgrades and Drawbacks]] table. You’re free to suggest your own creative ideas if the GM approves. If you earned two or more upgrades, you can (if you wish) instead choose from the next section, “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Advanced Item Features|Advanced Item Features.]]”

For each drawback earned, roll a d6 on the same table to randomly determine a drawback. The GM may also assign you a creative drawback in certain situations.

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Item Upgrades and Drawbacks]]

### Advanced Item Features

In exceptional circumstances, crafters can generate up to three upgrades during the crafting process. When this happens, you can choose to spend two upgrades to apply one feature of your choice from the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Advanced Item Features]] table, instead of choosing from the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Item Upgrades and Drawbacks]] table.

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Advanced Item Features]]

### Crafting Items With Nonstandard Materials

You can craft an item using **nonstandard materials**, such as wood, glass, obsidian, stone, or hide. Using a nonstandard material instead of metal makes your weapon, armor, or other item Allomantically inert, thus thwarting the effects of Iron Allomancy and Steel Allomancy (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/07 - Ch 6 Metallic Arts#Allomantically Inert Materials|Allomantically Inert Materials]]” in chapter 6).

Crafting items made of nonstandard materials requires more time and materials than crafting a standard item, but it otherwise follows the same steps. The GM may choose to apply the Fragile or Dangerous trait to represent the item’s volatility, or the item might lose some relevant weapon traits, armor traits, or expert traits. 

At the GM’s discretion, you can use nonstandard materials to craft any standard weapon, armor, ammunition, or other equipment that has a price (see the tables earlier in this chapter). Doing so follows the standard crafting rules in the previous “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Crafting Mundane Items|Crafting Mundane Items]]” section, but it typically costs 10 times as many boxings (thus increasing the crafting time as well). As usual, you must have an expertise relating to the item you’re creating (see the earlier “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Crafting Prerequisites|Crafting Prerequisites]]” section)—unless you’re crafting the standard hazekiller rounds described in the next section, which can be crafted without an expertise.

### Hazekiller Rounds Era 2

Most Allomancers are accustomed to having an upper hand in battle, but this can backfire when facing a marksperson with the right tools. Hazekiller rounds are bullets specially designed to trick, hinder, and harm enemy Allomancers who wield certain powers. These innovative rounds are a well-kept secret among the few who make and use them.

**Crafting Hazekiller Rounds.** You can craft the ammunition on the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Standard Hazekiller Rounds]] table even if you don’t have a relevant expertise. Unless you’re using rare materials, you can craft fifty hazekiller rounds of a particular type at a time, with a total material cost of 20 boxings. If you have the Munitions Crafting utility expertise, you can also experiment with inventing new hazekiller rounds and other hazekiller weapons. Work with the GM to determine how they work and if crafting them requires more expensive materials than the standard crafting rules account for.

**Special Calibration.** Mainstream firearms aren’t correctly calibrated for hazekiller rounds, so unless you have a special firearm, it’s risky to fire these bullets. When hazekiller rounds are used in a firearm without the Special Ammunition Calibration component (see the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Firearm Prototype Designs]] table at the end of this chapter), those attack tests gain a disadvantage, and that firearm temporarily gains the Fragile trait for those tests.

![[pg175_HB_Items_Exotic Ammunition_Anna Verhoog.webp]]

**Hazekiller rounds, each optimized to face a particular type of allomancer. - ANNA VERHOOG**

**Loading Hazekiller Rounds.** When you reload a firearm, you can choose to use one type of hazekiller round from your inventory (such as one from the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Standard Hazekiller Rounds]] table), instead of using your normal ammunition. When you do, you load a number of hazekiller rounds up to the Loaded value of that firearm. Your firearm then gains the effect of that hazekiller round until you reload again.

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Standard Hazekiller Rounds]]

### Crafting Aluminum Items Era 2

Aluminum is sought after for the protection it offers against the Metallic Arts. In Era 2, you can craft items with aluminum in place of other metals (for example, when creating a weapon that requires metal), or you can add it to non-metal items. See “[[Mistborn Handbook/07 - Ch 6 Metallic Arts#Allomantically Inert Materials|Allomantically Inert Materials]]” in chapter 6 for details on aluminum’s effects.

As listed earlier on the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Rare Metal Crafting Costs]] table, it costs 500 boxings to obtain aluminum for crafting. Alternatively, at the GM’s discretion, you might be able to salvage enough aluminum from another item. When crafting aluminum ammunition, 500 boxings’ worth of aluminum can produce 50 rounds of ammunition.

Aluminum can also be used to fortify structures, but such applications are prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive. If you wish to attempt something like lining your group’s hideout with sheets of aluminum, work with your GM to determine how costly this would be, and whether it’s possible for you to source that much aluminum.

## Crafting Metalminds

Metalminds must be crafted from metals that meet exacting standards for purity and alloy composition. If you’re a Feruchemist, Ferring, or Twinborn, you’ll typically receive your metalminds as rewards for completing the goal from your Metalborn path’s key talent (see “[[Mistborn Handbook/06 - Ch 5 Metalborn Paths#Metalborn Goals and Rewards|Metalborn Goals and Rewards]]” in chapter 5). When you gain a metalmind in that way, you don’t need to pay for that metal, and you don’t follow the standard crafting rules to make it.

However, if you lose your metalmind and need to recreate it (or if you want to create an additional metalmind for any reason), see the following sections.

### Recreating Lost Metalminds

If you lose an existing metalmind and need to recreate it, you can construct a new metalmind using the standard crafting rules. When you do so with a rare metal, the material cost equals the cost of the raw metal; if your metalmind is of a common metal, it costs 5 boxings to recreate. (Keep in mind that before crafting additional metalminds in this way, you must have already unlocked the corresponding power through a Metalborn goal and reward.)

Alternatively, you can add another “Construct Your Metalmind” goal to earn a metalmind as a reward, following the same process as you did to gain your first. When you do, your GM might also require you to source top-quality metal, either as a reward or by paying a steep price (see the earlier [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Rare Metal Crafting Costs]] table).

If you still have your metalmind but want to create a backup metalmind, you can use either of the above options as if you’d lost your metalmind; however, as described in “[[Mistborn Handbook/06 - Ch 5 Metalborn Paths#Metalborn Goals and Rewards|Metalborn Goals and Rewards]]” in chapter 5, you can’t simultaneously benefit from two of the same metalmind.

### Remaking Existing Metalminds

When you acquire a metalmind from your Metalborn path, you choose its form (such as a necklace or bracelet). If you later wish to remake it in a different form that has no mechanical benefit—such as a pocket watch or powder compact—you can do so during 1 day or more of downtime (at the GM’s discretion). When refashioning your existing metalmind into a decorative object of a similar size, you generally don’t need to procure additional metal or follow the standard crafting rules. (To integrate your metalmind into useful equipment, see “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Special Metalminds|Special Metalminds]]”).

### Special Metalminds

If you’re crafty enough, your metalmind can double as an otherwise useful piece of equipment. For example, crafting a brassmind in the form of knucklebones is perfect for heated close quarters combat. It might not be practical to construct an item entirely out of chromium, but a chromiummind plating on your firearm is stylish and fortuitous. Since a metalmind is an Invested item, it’s resistant to many forms of Invested interference, such as a Lurcher trying to Ironpull it away from you.

At the GM’s discretion, you can integrate an existing metalmind into nearly any item you’re crafting, often as a casing or embellishment. To do so, follow the usual crafting rules for creating that equipment, then spend two upgrades while crafting to choose the “Special Metalmind” advanced item feature.

## Inventing Firearms Era 2

Cutting-edge inventors often devote themselves to firearms, experimenting with never-before-seen prototypes or those that are otherwise found only in cloistered workshops. These experimental technologies might be optimized for anyone, whether a regular marksperson, Allomancer, Feruchemist, or hazekiller.

As with crafting other items, inventing firearm prototypes requires a relevant expertise. If you have an expertise in the specific firearm you’re crafting (such as the Revolver expertise), your firearm prototype has the same expert traits as its base firearm. If you only have the Munitions Crafting expertise, you can invent new firearms, but they won’t have the expert traits of the base firearm.

If you have a relevant expertise, use the following steps to craft your firearm prototype (instead of following the usual crafting rules):

1.  Choose a mundane firearm to model your invention after.

2.  Choose a prototype design to create.

3.  Acquire the necessary materials.

4.  Make a Crafting test.

5.  Apply any upgrades or drawbacks.

![[pg277_HB_Items_Bespoke Firearm_Anna Verhoog.webp]]

**A bespoke firearm - ANNA VERHOOG**

### Step 1: Choose Your Base Firearm

To invent a bespoke firearm, first you must decide which tried and true design to base it on. Choose a light weapon or heavy weapon from the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Weapons: Firearms]] table earlier in this chapter.

### Step 2: Choose Your Prototype Design

Depending on what utility you want to add to the base firearm, choose a prototype component from the upcoming [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Firearm Prototype Designs]] table. Each prototype component describes how it modifies the base weapon, lists any requirements you must meet to add it, and presents an optional upgrade and drawback.

### Step 3: Acquire Materials

Much like crafting other items, it’s assumed you can acquire most of the raw materials you need. The cost of these materials equals half the purchase price of the firearm you chose in step 1.

This material cost includes everything except rare metals (see [[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Step 1: Acquire Materials|step 1]] of “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Crafting Mundane Items|Crafting Mundane Items]]” for the costs of crafting with rare metals). If you want your firearm to be Allomantically inert, you can use aluminum instead of the base weapon’s usual metals (see the earlier “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items|Crafting Aluminum Items]]” section).

### Step 4: Make a Crafting Test

Once you’ve sourced your materials, you can make and resolve a Crafting test. This takes one day of downtime. However, this time can be modified by Opportunities or Complications during the crafting process: Each Opportunity you roll during your Crafting test reduces the time to create the item by 25 percent, while each Complication increases it by 25 percent.

Make a Crafting test and raise the stakes. Consult the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Firearm Crafting Test]] table to determine how your test result affects your crafting.

#### [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Firearm Crafting Test]]

### Step 5: Apply Upgrades and Drawbacks

For each upgrade you earned as part of your Crafting test, choose an upgrade listed next to your chosen prototype component (from the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Firearm Prototype Designs]] table) or any qualifying upgrade from the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Item Upgrades and Drawbacks]] table. If you earned two upgrades, you can instead choose (if you wish) from the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Advanced Item Features]] table in the “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Crafting Mundane Items|Crafting Mundane Items]]” section.

For each drawback you earned, you can choose the drawback listed next to your chosen prototype component, or you can randomly choose a drawback by rolling a d6 on the [[Mistborn Handbook/17 - Appendix 2 Tables|Item Upgrades and Drawbacks]] table in the “[[Mistborn Handbook/08 - Ch 7 Items#Crafting Mundane Items|Crafting Mundane Items]]” section. The GM may also assign you a creative drawback or come up with another creative idea for one of your Complications.
