# Feruchemist

*Sazed collapsed, death coming upon him like a cold shadow. And yet, the thought rang true. Ten rings… Weight. Speed of body. Sight. Hearing. Touch. Scent. Strength. Speed of mind. Wakefulness. And health. He tapped gold… His arm straightened, the bones reknitting as he drew upon several days’ worth of health in a brief flash of power.*

Full Feruchemists can use the metalminds of every Feruchemical metal, storing many of their traits in preparation for tapping them later. Thanks to the sheer variety of traits they can draw on, a Feruchemist can be a highly flexible and dynamic opponent, able to alter the flow of a confrontation in a blink. Against a more physically powerful opponent, they can tap strength from a pewtermind; a swifter opponent can be outmatched by tapping speed from a steelmind; and smarter opponents can be countered by tapping mental speed from a zincmind. Even Allomancers can struggle to gain the upper hand against a skilled Feruchemist; for example, an Ironpull or Steelpush can be thrown off by a Feruchemist suddenly tapping weight from their ironmind.

Yet all this power comes at a cost. Feruchemy only allows the tapping of traits that have already been painstakingly stored. A Feruchemist can rapidly drain an entire metalmind to achieve superhuman feats—but in so doing, they often consume days’ worth of storing in just a few moments. As a result, most Feruchemists spend nearly every waking moment (and for Bronze Feruchemy, even their sleeping moments) charging their many metalminds so they can always be prepared for danger.

## Feruchemist History

To the Terris people, Feruchemy is an existentially sacred power they’ve fought to preserve throughout Scadrial’s history. Allomancy only emerged when the Lord Ruler granted it to his loyalists at the start of his Final Empire reign; by contrast, Feruchemy arose naturally in the ancient days of Classical Scadrial.

Millennia ago, when the Shards Preservation and Ruin first arrived on Scadrial, their essences mingled. That power, composed equally of both of them, infused the ancient Scadrians. Thousands of years later, when the conflict between Preservation and Ruin began, the two Shards intentionally disentangled themselves. After that, the only known way to be a Feruchemist was by genetic inheritance.

In the centuries before the Lord Ruler’s Ascension, many Feruchemists joined the Worldbringers—a holy order of scholars who traveled Classical Scadrial, gifting the knowledge within their copperminds to all who would listen, while collecting the knowledge that was freely given.

When the Lord Ruler secured his grip on Scadrial and created the Final Empire, he feared the Feruchemical powers and sought to cleanse them from the population. He started by forcibly transforming all known Feruchemists into mistwraiths. Then he began brutal selective breeding programs, attempting to prevent anyone with recessive Feruchemist traits from passing them down.

The Terris people adapted and fought back against this atrocity in any way they could. In many ways, Feruchemy became both a symbol and product of Terris resilience and resistance. They tricked the program organizers into allowing hidden Feruchemist bloodlines to couple, then they protected and trained the children born with Feruchemy.

Inspired by the Worldbringers of old, these Feruchemists founded the Keepers. This clandestine organization’s mandate was to seek out the lost knowledge of Scadrial’s past and ensure its spread to future generations. All Keepers maintained vast and varied reserves of knowledge, but each also specialized in a particular area of study, such as classical religions or biographies of classical leaders. Through the thousand-year reign of the Final Empire, the Keepers persisted, outlasting even the seemingly immortal Lord Ruler.

By Era 2, few if any full Feruchemists remained. The Feruchemical powers diluted among the populace over time (as did the Allomantic powers), especially after the deregulation of Terris breeding. It’s believed that nearly every Scadrian in Era 2 has Feruchemy in their lineage. This gave rise to a new type of Feruchemist: the Ferring, a person who wields only one Feruchemical power rather than all of them. However, some of the Terris elite continued to collaborate in an attempt to recreate full Feruchemy.

## Building a Feruchemist

Playing a Feruchemist can be intimidating due to the sheer number of Feruchemical powers and the need to track each metalmind separately. If you’re up for the challenge, the following “[[Mistborn Handbook/06 - Ch 5 Metalborn Paths#Iconic Feruchemist|Iconic Feruchemist]]” section provides an example build for characters inspired by the novels. Also consider these general tips for building an effective Feruchemist:

**Attributes.** To increase your **Feruchemy** score and increase the number of metalminds you can wear, prioritize the **Intellect** attribute. Your other attributes largely depend on the first Feruchemical powers you plan to construct metalminds for. For example, powers that store a particular attribute (like [[Metallic Arts/Electrum/Electrum Feruchemy|Electrum Feruchemy]]) typically benefit from investing in that attribute; if your powers lend themself to combat (like [[Metallic Arts/Steel/Steel Feruchemy|Steel Feruchemy]]), consider **Strength** or **Speed** to empower your weapon attacks.

**Heroic Paths.** Many Feruchemists not only invest in their Metalborn path and its powers, but they supplement it with heroic talents. It’s a good idea to consider the build advice from any heroic paths of interest.

**Skills.** Most Feruchemists prioritize ranks in **Feruchemy**. Also consider investing in mundane skills, such as those that are prerequisites for Metalborn talents or heroic talents you’re interested in.

## Feruchemist Key Talent

Terris people can inherit a genetic trait that grants them Feruchemical powers. The Final Empire hunts Feruchemists in a despicable effort to prevent them from passing on this heritage, so Terris people commonly avoid experimenting to see if they have Feruchemical powers. Choosing this talent represents your character discovering their powers and unlocks access to the Feruchemist tree.

**Starting Skill: None.** If you choose Feruchemist as your starting path, you don’t gain a free rank in a skill.
